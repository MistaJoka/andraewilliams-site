## Distance is a decision, not a position

Amateurs think of range as where they are standing. Technical fighters
think of it as which exchanges are currently available — to both people.
Stepping in is not "getting closer", it is opening a menu and handing the
other person a copy.

## Angles beat speed

A straight retreat keeps you in the same line of fire and loses ground you
have to win back. A step off the line changes what your opponent is
looking at, and it costs the same energy.

Speed wins the first exchange. Angles win the fourth, because your
opponent is now hitting where you were.

## Timing is reading intent

You cannot react to a punch that is already travelling — the distance is
too short and the clock is too fast. What technical fighters react to is
the *setup*: the weight shift, the guard drop, the step that has to happen
before the strike does.

That is why film study works and reflex training plateaus. You are not
training a faster hand. You are training an earlier read.

## The pattern

All three are the same skill in different clothes: acting on the
precondition rather than the event. It is the same reason the fraud rules
that work look at sequences instead of transactions.
