## What holds up

**Information density with clear hierarchy.** The good ones are dense but
never uniform — one primary readout, several secondary, and a lot of
deliberately unreadable chrome that exists to give the real data
somewhere to sit against.

**Monospace and uppercase for machine voice.** It reads as *system*
rather than *person*, and it does so instantly, without a label saying so.

**State as motion.** A value that changed sweeps or flickers once. That
is far more legible than a colour swap, and it works for people who
cannot distinguish the colours.

## What does not survive contact

**Text that is never meant to be read.** On screen it is texture. In a
real interface someone will try to read it, fail, and conclude the
product is broken.

**Constant ambient animation.** It sells "live system" in a two-second
shot and becomes unbearable in minute three. Motion has to be tied to a
state change or it is noise.

**Extreme low contrast for mood.** Looks cinematic, fails everyone using
the thing in daylight.

## The transferable rule

Borrow the *structure* — hierarchy, machine voice, motion tied to state —
and leave the *atmosphere*. The atmosphere is doing a job in a film that
it cannot do in a tool: a film knows exactly how long you will look at the
frame, and an interface does not.
