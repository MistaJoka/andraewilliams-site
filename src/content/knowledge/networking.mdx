<WhyItMatters>
You just watched one device answer a question it had no right to answer, and traffic reroute through it without asking permission. That's not unique to ARP. A forged DNS reply at Level 4, a rogue DHCP or routing announcement at Level 2, a fake Wi-Fi access point at Level 0 — all the same trick on a different layer: get between two honest parties before they notice, by being fast and unauthenticated.

Every real defense against this class of attack is really just adding authentication somewhere the stack previously had none. TLS proves the server is who it claims to be. 802.1X gates who's even allowed to plug into the switch. DNSSEC proves a DNS answer wasn't forged in transit. None of it stops an attacker from being on the network. All of it stops an attacker from being trusted once they are.
</WhyItMatters>
