## What it looks like

The savage's working environment is a product they maintain. Every tool in it was chosen deliberately, is known deeply, and can be rebuilt from scratch on a new machine in under an hour from a versioned configuration. Keyboard over mouse. Text over clicks. Scripts over memory.

Repetition is the enemy. Anything done twice gets a note; anything done three times gets automated — a script, a template, a checklist, a jig, a macro. The savage's personal library of these grows every month and is the real reason they move at two or three times the speed of a peer with the same knowledge.

Workflow is explicit. There is a fixed shape to how work enters, moves, and leaves: intake, plan, build, verify, ship, record. The shape is written down and followed even on small jobs, because the small jobs are where sloppiness is rehearsed. Backups, version control, and recovery are boring because they are tested.

## The gap

The gap looks like a machine that would take a week to recover if it died tonight. It looks like the same ten commands typed by hand every day for a decade. It looks like a new tool adopted every month and none of them mastered. It looks like a workflow that lives only in the savage's head, so nobody can cover for them and they cannot cover for themselves on a bad day.

## How to get there

- Put every configuration file, script, and template under version control. Today. Rebuild a clean machine from it once a year and fix what breaks.
- Keep a rule-of-three log: second occurrence gets written down, third gets automated. Review the log monthly.
- Learn one tool to depth per quarter — the editor, the shell, the version control system, the primary instrument of the craft. Read its manual end to end.
- Write the workflow down as a one-page checklist. Use it on the next five jobs without deviation, then revise it.
- Test restore, not just backup. A backup never restored is a hope.
- Measure the loop: time from idea to verified result on a trivial change. Cut it in half, then again.

## Measures

- Full environment reproducible from versioned config; last clean rebuild dated within twelve months.
- Automation library: count of scripts, templates, and checklists in use, and at least twelve added in the last year.
- Written workflow checklist exists and was used on the last five jobs: yes or no.
- Restore from backup tested and dated within the last ninety days.
- Idea-to-verified-result loop time on a trivial change, measured and recorded, under five minutes.

## Canon

- **The Pragmatic Programmer**, David Thomas & Andrew Hunt — automation-first craftsmanship and the case for owning your toolchain instead of borrowing one.
- **The Unix Programming Environment**, Brian Kernighan & Rob Pike — the philosophy of small, composable tools this workflow discipline descends from.
- **The Phoenix Project**, Gene Kim, Kevin Behr & George Spafford — treating workflow as a visible, engineered system rather than tribal habit.
- **Getting Things Done**, David Allen — capturing and systematizing repeated work so nothing depends on memory.
