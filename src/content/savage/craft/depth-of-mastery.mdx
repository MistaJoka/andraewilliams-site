## What it looks like

The savage has one core subject they know better than anyone they are likely to meet, and they know it from the bottom up. Not the framework — the thing the framework is built on. A builder knows the protocol under the library. An analyst knows the maths under the model. A surgeon knows the anatomy under the procedure. When the abstraction leaks, they go one layer down without flinching.

Mastery shows as speed of diagnosis. A problem others spend a day on is placed in minutes, because the savage has seen its shape a hundred times and can name the three likely causes before opening anything. It shows as calm when the tooling fails: they can do the work by hand, slower, but correctly.

It is earned, not declared. Ten thousand hours is the folk number; the real measure is hours of deliberate, corrected practice on the hard parts, not hours of repeating the easy ones. The savage has at least one domain at that level and is two years into building the next (see CRF·05).

## The gap

The gap looks like ten years of experience that is one year repeated ten times. It looks like fluency in a tool and panic when the tool changes. It looks like the word "senior" on a profile and an inability to explain why the system does what it does. It looks like copying the answer without being able to derive it, and defending the copy when it is questioned.

## How to get there

- Name the one subject. Write it down. Everything else is adjacent.
- Go one layer down, on purpose: read the specification, the source, the original paper, the standard — not the summary of it.
- Rebuild the core thing from scratch once. A parser, a ledger, a model, a joint. Whatever the craft's atom is, make one by hand.
- Keep a written log of every problem that took more than an hour: the cause, the fix, the general rule. Reread it quarterly.
- Seek correction from someone better, at least monthly. Pay for it if necessary.
- Practise the hard parts at the edge of failure, not the comfortable parts for the feeling of progress.

## Measures

- The core subject is written down in one sentence, and has not changed in the last twelve months.
- Can explain the layer beneath the daily tool to a peer, unprompted, without notes: yes or no.
- Problem log exists, with at least twenty dated entries in the last year.
- At least one from-scratch rebuild of the craft's core artefact, finished and dated.
- Received structured correction from someone better at least twelve times in the last year, logged.
