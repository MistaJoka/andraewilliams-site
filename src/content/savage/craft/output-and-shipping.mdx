## What it looks like

The savage ships. Not starts, not explores, not "is working on" — ships. Every piece of work has a name, a definition of done, and a date, all set before the work begins. When the date arrives, the work is out: released, delivered, published, handed over. Scope moves; the date does not.

Output is visible. Over any twelve-month window there is a list of finished things a stranger could inspect, and it is long. In software, that is releases and merged work; in analysis, delivered reports with decisions attached; in a trade, completed jobs signed off by the client. The unit differs by craft; the cadence does not. Something finished leaves the savage's hands every week.

Finished means usable by someone who is not you. Documented enough to run. Tested enough to trust. Handed over so cleanly that the next person needs no conversation to pick it up.

## The gap

The gap looks like the half-built side project, the ninety-percent-done report, the branch that has been "nearly ready" for a month. It looks like polishing the part nobody asked for while the deliverable slips. It looks like a backlog of ideas and a short list of results. It looks like calling research "work" because it feels like work.

## How to get there

- Before starting anything, write three lines: what done looks like, the ship date, and the one thing that would make you kill it. No lines, no start.
- Cut scope, never date. If the date is at risk, the deliverable shrinks that day — out loud.
- Ship the smallest thing that is complete, then iterate in public. Version two is allowed to exist only because version one shipped.
- Keep a running ledger of shipped work with dates. Review it monthly; a month with nothing in it is a red flag, not a rest.
- Limit work in progress to two items. A third cannot start until one ships or is killed.
- Kill cleanly. Abandoned work gets a dated note saying why. Zombies are not allowed.

## Measures

- Shipped ledger exists; at least fifty dated entries in the last twelve months.
- No calendar month in the last year with zero shipped items.
- Every active piece of work has a written done-definition, ship date, and kill criterion: yes or no.
- Work in progress right now: two or fewer.
- Ratio of dates hit to dates set, last twelve months: 80% or higher, with every miss annotated.

## Canon

- **The Mythical Man-Month**, Frederick P. Brooks Jr. — the classic case for cutting scope, not moving the date, when a deliverable is at risk.
- **Shape Up**, Ryan Singer — fixed time, variable scope, and the appetite-based method for shipping the smallest complete thing on a set date.
- **The Effective Executive**, Peter Drucker — the discipline of finishing what matters instead of merely being busy.
- **The Lean Startup**, Eric Ries — build-measure-learn as the case for shipping small and iterating in public rather than polishing in private.
