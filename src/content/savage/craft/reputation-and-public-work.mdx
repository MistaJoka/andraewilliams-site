## What it looks like

The savage works in the open. Finished things go out under their real name: repositories, published analysis, talks, portfolios, case studies, whatever the craft's public artefact is. Anyone who searches the name finds the work first, and the work answers the question "are they good?" before the savage is in the room.

Reputation is built on claims that can be checked. Every public statement of skill has a public artefact behind it. The savage does not describe their craft; they point at it. Recognition arrives as inbound: people asking for help, for work, for opinions, because they saw the thing.

Public work is also the sharpest form of verification. Errors get found. The savage counts that as a feature, corrects in public, and credits the person who caught it. A public correction handled well is worth more than a private success nobody saw.

## The gap

The gap looks like a decade of good work locked inside employers' walls with nothing to show for it. It looks like a profile full of adjectives and no links. It looks like fear of being wrong in public, which means never being right in public either. It looks like a body of work that exists only in the savage's memory and a reputation that resets every time they change jobs.

## How to get there

- Own the name: a site, a canonical profile, one place everything links back to. The savage controls it, no platform does.
- Publish one finished thing a month. A tool, an essay, a result, a teardown. Small and finished beats large and private.
- Every shipped item (see CRF·02) gets the question: can any part of this go public? Default yes.
- Write the case study within a week of finishing significant work, while the lessons are sharp.
- Correct errors in public within 24 hours of learning of them. Credit the finder.
- Say no to work that cannot be named, unless it pays enough to buy the public work it displaces.

## Measures

- Searching the savage's name returns their own work in the top three results: yes or no, checked quarterly.
- Public artefacts published in the last twelve months: twelve or more, dated.
- Inbound requests — for work, help, or opinion — triggered by public work in the last year, counted.
- Every public claim of skill links to an artefact that proves it: audit done and dated within six months.
- Public corrections issued within 24 hours of discovery, last year: 100% of known errors.
