## What it looks like

The savage holds a current first aid and CPR/AED certification from a recognised body — not a video watched once, but a course with a manikin, an instructor, and a card that expires. It is renewed before it lapses, every two years, for life.

Competence is specific. You can run CPR at the right depth and rate for ten minutes without stopping. You can control serious bleeding with direct pressure and a commercial tourniquet, and you have practised the tourniquet on your own limb. You recognise a stroke, a heart attack, anaphylaxis, a choking adult and a choking infant, and you know the next action for each without consulting a phone.

The kit matches the competence. There is one in the home, one in the car, one in the bag that goes on trips. Each contains what you have been trained to use and nothing decorative. Expiry dates are checked on a schedule.

The savage also knows their own medical facts cold: blood type, allergies, medications, emergency contacts — on a card in the wallet and on the phone lock screen.

## The gap

The gap is a certification from a job eight years ago. It is a kit full of plasters and nothing for a bleed. It is the confident assumption that you would "figure it out," which in practice means standing in a circle filming while someone's brain goes without oxygen.

## How to get there

- Book a blended or in-person first aid + CPR/AED course this month. Eight hours, a manikin, a certificate. Put the renewal date in the calendar before you leave the room.
- Take a bleeding-control course — a dedicated two-hour session exists in most cities. Buy the tourniquet the instructor recommends, not the cheapest one online.
- Build three kits: home, vehicle, travel. Inventory each on a checklist; audit quarterly.
- Drill monthly. Ten minutes: CPR on a cushion to a metronome at 100–120 bpm, tourniquet on your own thigh in under thirty seconds, recite the stroke and choking protocols aloud.
- Keep conditioning (BDY·02) high — ten minutes of effective compressions is a physical event.

## Measures

- First aid and CPR/AED certificate: valid today, issuer and expiry date written down.
- Tourniquet applied one-handed to own arm in under 30 seconds, timed, within the last 90 days.
- Three kits exist; last inventory audit dated within the last 90 days; zero expired items.
- Emergency medical card in wallet and on phone lock screen: yes/no.
- Monthly drill logged for each of the last 12 months.

## Canon

- **Where There Is No Doctor**, David Werner — the classic global reference for delivering real medical care when no doctor is present.
- **The Survival Medicine Handbook**, Joseph Alton & Amy Alton — a modern, comprehensive reference for treating serious injury and illness when professional care is delayed.
- **NOLS Wilderness Medicine**, Tod Schimelpfenig — the standard text behind wilderness first aid certification and field trauma decision-making.
- **American Red Cross First Aid/CPR/AED Participant's Manual** — the official curriculum behind the certification this standard requires.
