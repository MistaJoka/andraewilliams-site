## What it looks like

The savage owns a tool set that covers every ordinary repair and knows where each tool is without looking. Not a drawer of mismatched bits: a socket set, a drill and driver with bits for wood, masonry and metal, a multimeter, an adjustable wrench, a stud finder, a proper level, a torque wrench for the car, and consumables stocked before they run out.

Fluency spans the basic trades. Plumbing: replace a tap cartridge, clear a trap, reseat a toilet, stop a leak and know where the main shutoff is in any building you sleep in. Electrical: change a fixture or outlet safely with the breaker off and the circuit tested dead; know the limits the law and your competence place on the rest. Carpentry: hang a door, fix a hinge, patch drywall invisibly, build a square frame. Mechanical: change a tyre in ten minutes, jump a battery, swap wipers, brake pads and oil, read a fault code.

The savage diagnoses before acting. Watch, listen, isolate, test one variable at a time. When the job is genuinely beyond the line — gas, structural, high-voltage — the savage knows that in advance and says so, then watches the professional work.

## The gap

The gap is a dead bulb that stayed dead for a month. It is a dripping tap costing water for a year. It is calling a plumber to change a washer, or a tyre shop for a flat. It is a warning light that has been on so long it became furniture.

## How to get there

- Inventory the tools. Buy the missing ones once, at a quality that outlives you. Give every tool a home.
- Walk the home and the car. List every repair skill each could demand in a year. Learn one per month from a manual, a tradesperson, or a short course — then do it with your own hands before it is needed.
- Fix the next five broken things yourself before calling anyone. Log what you learned.
- Do your own routine car maintenance for a year: tyre rotation, oil, filters, wipers, pads. Own the torque specs.
- Locate and label the water shutoff, gas shutoff, and breaker panel in every place you live; teach them to whoever lives there.

## Measures

- Tool inventory list exists; every listed tool locatable within 60 seconds.
- Flat tyre to driving: under 10 minutes, timed in the last year.
- Repairs completed personally in the last 12 months: count, logged with dates.
- Open repair backlog in home and vehicle: zero items older than 14 days.
- Shutoffs and breaker panel labelled; household members can point to each: yes/no.
