## What it looks like

The savage's home is provisioned for a 72-hour disruption without shopping, power or running water. Water is the first line: four litres per person per day for three days, stored and rotated, plus a filter or purification method for longer. Food: three days of no-cook calories everyone in the house will actually eat, rotated into the normal pantry. Power: a charged power bank for every phone, a larger battery or generator sized to the essentials. Light and heat: headlamps with spare batteries, a safe way to stay warm if the heating fails.

Documents are together: identification, insurance, deeds, medical records, in a fire-resistant box and encrypted offsite. Cash exists in small notes, enough for a week when the cards stop working. A full tank is the standing rule — the car never drops below half.

There is a written plan. Where the family meets if the home is unusable. Who the out-of-area contact is. What each person carries. What goes in the bag if you have five minutes. The plan covers the hazards that apply to your address and has been walked through with everyone, including the children.

The small disruptions are covered too: a spare key with a trusted person, a month of any essential medication, backups of every file that matters.

## The gap

The gap is a flashlight with dead batteries and a "we'd just go to my parents'" plan that assumes the roads are open. It is a cupboard with one tin of beans and a kettle that needs the grid. It is a household where one person knows where the documents are and that person is not home.

## How to get there

- List the realistic hazards for your address. Build the plan for those first.
- Store water this week. Then food, then power, then light and heat. One category per week for six weeks.
- Assemble a go-bag per person: documents copies, water, food, light, first aid (SRV·01), clothing, cash, chargers. Keep it by the door.
- Write the plan on one page. Post it. Walk through it with the household twice a year.
- Run the drill: kill the mains breaker for an evening and live off the kit. Fix what failed.

## Measures

- Stored water: at least 12 litres per person, rotation date within the last 6 months.
- 72 hours of no-cook food per person, with expiry dates logged: yes/no.
- Every household phone has a charged power bank: tested monthly.
- Written plan exists, dated, reviewed within the last 6 months; every household member can state the meeting point.
- Blackout drill completed within the last 12 months, with a list of what failed and what was fixed.
- Car fuel never below half: yes/no, checked weekly.
