## What it looks like

The savage can read a topographic map, orient it with a compass, take and follow a bearing, and estimate distance by pace count and time. Dropped in unfamiliar terrain with a map, the savage walks out. Dropped in a city with a dead phone, the savage finds north by the sun, reads street numbering, and walks to the destination.

Every place the savage lives or regularly visits has a mental map: three routes home from work, the hospital, the police station, the nearest water, the direction of the nearest high ground. In a new city, the first evening is spent with a paper map learning the grid.

Behind the wheel, the savage drives at an advanced standard: smooth, anticipating, unbothered by rain, snow, night, or a motorway at speed. Skid control has been practised on a course, not discovered on a road. The savage can also ride a bicycle in traffic, handle a manual gearbox, and move confidently on foot over rough ground for a full day — 25 km with a pack, without injury.

Navigation extends to transport systems. Any train, bus or ferry network can be worked out from the posted map in minutes.

## The gap

The gap is arriving in a city you have visited ten times and still needing the blue dot. It is not knowing which direction your own front door faces. It is a driver who has never braked hard, never driven on ice, and has no idea what the car does at the limit. It is being unable to name three routes home.

## How to get there

- Take a one-day map-and-compass course or learn from a manual, then practise monthly on a trail with the phone in the pack, off.
- Learn the home city from a paper map: grid, landmarks, cardinal directions. Name the three routes home aloud and drive each one.
- Book an advanced or defensive driving course with a skid-pan element. Repeat every five years.
- Walk and cycle the city until its layout is in the body, not the phone. Navigate on foot with the phone off once a week.
- Build the distance: one long walk per month with a loaded pack, up to 25 km.

## Measures

- Map-and-compass navigation exercise completed, phone off, within the last 90 days.
- Three routes home from work: named without aids, yes/no.
- Advanced driving course: certificate dated within the last five years.
- 25 km loaded walk completed within the last 12 months, dated.
- Can point north from home without looking: yes/no, tested by someone else.

## Canon

- **Be Expert with Map and Compass**, Björn Kjellström — the classic instructional text behind traditional orienteering and bearing-taking.
- **The Natural Navigator**, Tristan Gooley — reading sun, stars, and terrain to navigate without instruments.
- **The Lost Art of Finding Our Way**, John Edward Huth — the scientific case for why instrument-free navigation still matters.
- **Roadcraft: The Police Driver's Handbook** — the official manual behind advanced, anticipatory driving standards.
