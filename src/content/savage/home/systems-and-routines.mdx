## What it looks like

The savage does not decide how the morning goes. It is already decided. Wake at the same time seven days a week, within thirty minutes. The first hour has a fixed sequence — water, light, movement, hygiene, the first real task — and it is the same on a Tuesday as on holiday. No phone until the sequence is complete.

The evening has its mirror. The home is reset to standard (HME·01). Tomorrow is laid out: clothes, bag, the first task written down, the calendar checked. Screens go dark at a fixed hour. Lights out at a fixed hour. The routine is short enough to be kept when tired and strong enough to survive travel.

The week has a reset too. One fixed block — ninety minutes on a set day — in which everything that drifted is put back: inbox to zero, calendar for the coming week reviewed, finances glanced at, laundry and provisions done, the repairs list checked, the plan for the week written. After the weekly reset, nothing in life is unknown or overdue.

These systems are written down. Not in the head — on paper or in a file that could be handed to someone else. Routines that live only in memory die the first week life gets hard.

## The gap

The gap looks like a morning that starts with a phone and ends whenever it ends. It looks like "I'm not a morning person" used to excuse no routine at all. It looks like an evening that dissolves into a screen and a bedtime decided by exhaustion. It looks like a weekly reset that happens when things are bad enough to force it. It looks like systems that last exactly as long as motivation does.

## How to get there

- Write the morning sequence as a numbered list of no more than eight steps. Run it for thirty consecutive days before changing anything.
- Fix the wake time first; everything else follows from it. Same time on weekends.
- Build the evening routine backwards from lights out. Screens off sixty minutes before.
- Book the weekly reset in the calendar as a recurring appointment. Treat it as you would a client meeting.
- Track adherence with a simple mark per day. A streak broken is restarted the next day, not the next month.

## Measures

- Wake time variance across the last fourteen days: within thirty minutes, logged.
- Morning and evening sequences exist as written documents. Yes or no.
- Morning routine completed before first phone use: days out of the last thirty.
- Weekly reset completed in the last seven days, dated. Yes or no.
- Nothing overdue, unknown, or unplanned for the coming week after the reset. Yes or no.
