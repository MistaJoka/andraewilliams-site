## What it looks like

Readiness is the proof that the rest of this domain is real. The savage keeps a bag packed at all times: three days of clothing for the season, toiletries, chargers and a charged power bank, a first-aid kit, a torch, a printed list of key contacts, photocopies of identity documents, and enough cash to cover fuel, food, and a room. It lives by the door or in the car. It is checked and re-packed on the first of every quarter.

The vehicle is never below a quarter tank, and it carries its own kit: jump leads, a tyre inflator, a blanket, water, a basic tool roll, a torch, and a first-aid kit. It is maintained on schedule (HME·03), so the question of whether it will start does not exist.

The household has a written plan. What to do in a fire, a flood, a power outage of three days, a medical emergency, a family member unreachable. Where to meet. Who to call, in what order. Where the documents are (HME·04), where the water is (HME·06), where the shut-offs are for gas, water, and electricity. Everyone in the household knows the plan because it has been walked through, not just written.

The home is ready for the lights to go out: torches with charged batteries at known points, a way to cook without mains power, a way to keep warm, a charged radio, a week of any essential medication. None of it is elaborate. All of it is present.

## The gap

The gap looks like packing in a panic at 2 a.m. because of a phone call. It looks like a car on empty the night it is needed. It looks like not knowing where the water shut-off is until the ceiling is coming down. It looks like a first-aid kit with one plaster and a dead torch. It looks like a family that has never discussed where to meet. It looks like the belief that nothing will happen, held right up until it does.

## How to get there

- Pack the bag this weekend. Use a written list, weigh it, and put the re-pack date in the calendar.
- Adopt the quarter-tank rule today. Refuel when the gauge hits a quarter, every time, no exceptions.
- Assemble the car kit in one shop. Check it during the monthly vehicle walk-around.
- Write the household plan in two pages. Walk every member through it, including where the shut-offs are. Review annually.
- Stage power-outage kit at fixed points. Test torches and charge power banks at the quarterly re-pack.

## Measures

- Bag packed, by the door or in the car, last re-packed within ninety days, dated. Yes or no.
- Fuel gauge right now: at or above a quarter.
- Every household member can name the meeting point and locate the gas, water, and electricity shut-offs. Tested, yes or no.
- Written emergency plan exists, reviewed within twelve months, dated.
- Time to leave the house with bag, documents, and keys: under five minutes, timed.

## Canon

- **SAS Survival Handbook**, John "Lofty" Wiseman — the standard field reference for what a real emergency kit and plan need to cover.
- **Deep Survival: Who Lives, Who Dies, and Why**, Laurence Gonzales — the psychology of readiness, and why preparation beats improvisation when it counts.
- **When All Hell Breaks Loose**, Cody Lundin — practical, unglamorous preparation for the outages and disruptions actually likely to happen.
