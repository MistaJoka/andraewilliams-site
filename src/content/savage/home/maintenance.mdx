## What it looks like

The savage owns a maintenance calendar, and it is followed. Every asset with a service interval is on it: the vehicle, the heating and cooling, the water heater, the smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, the gutters, the bike, the tools, the boots. Each has a date and a record of the last time it was done.

The vehicle is serviced at or before the manufacturer's interval, never after. Oil, filters, brakes, tyres, fluids, and wipers are checked on a monthly walk-around that takes ten minutes. Tyre pressure is checked monthly and before any long trip. The tank is never below a quarter (HME·07). Registration, insurance, and inspection are never a surprise.

The home gets a seasonal pass — four times a year, a written checklist: filters, detectors, seals, drains, gutters, outdoor equipment, stored water, anything with a battery. Annual tasks — the boiler service, the chimney, the roof inspection — are booked the day the previous one is completed.

Gear is cleaned and inspected after use, not before the next use. Knives are sharp. Boots are conditioned. Batteries are charged. Anything that would fail under load is found on the bench, not in the field.

## The gap

The gap looks like a warning light ignored for a month. It looks like a furnace that fails in the first cold week because the filter was last changed by the previous owner. It looks like a dead smoke alarm chirping for days. It looks like a tyre at half pressure for a season. It looks like maintenance done reactively, at twice the cost and at the worst possible time, every single time. It looks like not knowing when the last service was because there is no record.

## How to get there

- Build the calendar once: list every asset, find its service interval, put every task on a recurring date. Two hours, done for good.
- Monthly vehicle walk-around: fluids, tyres, lights, wipers, horn. Ten minutes, first weekend of the month.
- Seasonal home checklist, printed, ticked, dated, kept.
- Book the next service before leaving the current one.
- Keep a single maintenance log — one file or notebook — with date, asset, what was done, and what it cost.

## Measures

- Maintenance calendar exists, covers every asset with an interval. Yes or no.
- Vehicle: last service date and mileage known without looking it up; next one booked.
- Smoke and CO alarms tested in the last thirty days, dated.
- Seasonal checklist completed within the last ninety days, dated.
- Unplanned failures in the last twelve months that a scheduled check would have caught: zero.

## Canon

- **Reliability-Centered Maintenance**, John Moubray — the industrial logic of scheduled, condition-based maintenance, applied here at household scale.
- **Shop Class as Soulcraft**, Matthew Crawford — the case for hands-on competence and caring for what you own as a form of respect for it.
- **Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance**, Robert Pirsig — maintenance as attention and quality of care, not a chore to be minimised.
