## What it looks like

The savage's home is ready without warning. Surfaces are clear. The bed is made before the room is left. Dishes do not sleep in the sink. Floors are swept or vacuumed weekly at minimum and spot-cleaned the moment they need it. The bathroom is a place you would let a surgeon scrub in.

Every object has one home, and it is there when it is not in use. There is no junk drawer, no chair that collects clothes, no corner where boxes go to die. What is owned is used, and what is not used in a year is given away, sold, or binned. The number of possessions is small enough that the whole place can be reset to standard in under thirty minutes.

Nothing stays broken. A dripping tap, a loose hinge, a blown bulb, a sticking drawer — each is fixed or scheduled within seven days of being noticed. Walls are unmarked, paint is intact, and nothing is held together with tape.

The space is also deliberate. Lighting suits the task. Air is fresh — windows opened daily, filters changed on schedule. There is one place to work, one place to rest, and they are not the same place.

## The gap

The gap looks like a place that is "fine" for you and a scramble before anyone visits. It looks like a clean kitchen and a bedroom nobody is allowed into. It looks like three things broken for six months because each one is small. It looks like owning so much that tidying means moving piles from one surface to another. It looks like a home that drains you instead of restoring you, and not noticing because you have never lived in the alternative.

## How to get there

- Reset to standard every night: ten minutes, same order, every room. Done before sitting down (HME·02).
- One place for everything. If an object has no place, it gets one or it leaves.
- Cull quarterly. One box out of every room, every quarter, without exception.
- Keep a running repairs list. Anything on it for more than seven days is either fixed that weekend or booked with a tradesperson (HME·03).
- Clean as the last step of every activity — cooking ends when the kitchen is clean, not when the food is eaten.

## Measures

- Could a guest arrive in the next five minutes, any day of the year, with nothing to hide? Yes or no.
- Full reset time from "lived in" to standard: under thirty minutes, timed.
- Repairs list: zero items older than seven days.
- Quarterly cull: dated log, at least one box out per room.
- Bed made, sink empty, surfaces clear: checked tonight, yes or no.

## Canon

- **The Life-Changing Magic of Tidying Up**, Marie Kondo — the case for one home per object and keeping only what earns its place.
- **Goodbye, Things**, Fumio Sasaki — a lived account of how little a fully reset space actually requires.
- **A Pattern Language**, Christopher Alexander, Sara Ishikawa & Murray Silverstein — the foundational text on what makes a physical space work for how people actually live in it.
- **The Gentle Art of Swedish Death Cleaning**, Margareta Magnusson — decluttering as an ongoing discipline, not a once-off event.
