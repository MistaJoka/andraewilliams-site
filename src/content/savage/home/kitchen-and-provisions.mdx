## What it looks like

The savage's kitchen is a tool, and it is kept like one. Knives are sharp. Pans are seasoned or unscratched. Every appliance works. Counters are clear and the sink is empty at the end of every day (HME·01). The fridge is cleaned and audited weekly, and nothing in it is a science experiment.

The pantry is stocked on a system, not on impulse. A standing list of staples — grains, legumes, oils, tinned goods, spices, frozen protein and vegetables — is held at a fixed level and restocked when it drops below it. Stock is rotated: first in, first out, oldest at the front. There is no item more than a year past its date.

From that pantry the savage can cook at least ten real meals from memory, without a recipe and without a shop. Cooking is daily, not occasional. Meals are planned for the week during the weekly reset (HME·02), and a single shop covers them. Food waste is near zero because what is bought is eaten.

There is a buffer. Two weeks of shelf-stable food for every person in the household, and water — at least four litres per person per day for three days at the minimum, more where it can be stored — with a means to purify more. This stock is part of the rotation, not a dusty box; it is eaten and replaced.

## The gap

The gap looks like a fridge with condiments and nothing else. It looks like takeaway four nights a week because there was "nothing in". It looks like a blunt knife, a broken oven light, and a drawer of unidentified utensils. It looks like a bag of rice from three addresses ago. It looks like a household that would be hungry in forty-eight hours if the shops closed. It looks like spending a fortune on food and throwing a third of it away.

## How to get there

- Write the staples list with a minimum quantity for each item. Tape it inside a cupboard door. Restock to the line, every week.
- Learn ten meals cold. Cook each one until it needs no recipe.
- Weekly: plan, shop once, prep what can be prepped, audit the fridge, bin what is gone.
- Sharpen knives monthly. Deep-clean the oven, fridge, and freezer quarterly.
- Build the two-week buffer over two months by buying one extra of each staple per shop. Date everything. Eat from it and replace it.

## Measures

- Ten meals cookable from the pantry tonight, without a shop, named on paper. Yes or no.
- Staples list exists with minimum quantities; every item at or above minimum, checked this week.
- Expired items in pantry, fridge, or freezer at the weekly audit: zero.
- Stored food and water: two weeks' food and three days' water per person, dated, rotated within the last year.
- Takeaway or delivery meals in the last seven days: fewer than two.
