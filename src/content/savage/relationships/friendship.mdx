## What it looks like

The savage has a handful of friendships — three to five — that have been maintained through moves, marriages, children, and bad years. These are people who have seen you fail and stayed. You know their partner's name, their parents' health, and what they are afraid of this year.

It looks like initiative. The savage is the one who calls, who books the trip, who turns "we should get together" into a date and an address. Friendships are not left to drift on the assumption that they will be there when needed. They are fed on a schedule.

It looks like generosity without accounting. You help them move. You show up to the hospital. You lend the money and forget it. You say the hard thing a friend needs to hear — about the drinking, the partner, the business — once, clearly, and then stay their friend whatever they decide.

## The gap

The gap looks like a contacts list of four hundred and nobody to call at 2 a.m. It looks like friendships that exist only in a group chat that has gone quiet. It looks like waiting to be invited, then resenting that you were not. It looks like every friend being from one era — school, one job — and none made since.

## How to get there

- Name the inner circle. Write the three to five names down. Those people get a real conversation every two weeks and an in-person meeting every quarter, distance permitting.
- Make the plan. Every "let's catch up" you send or receive becomes a calendar entry within a week or gets dropped.
- Book one standing annual event with close friends — a trip, a weekend, a fixed date — and hold it for a decade.
- Make one new friend a year. That means ten conversations with a stranger you chose to pursue, not an accident.
- Show up to their events: the wedding, the funeral, the launch. Attendance is the cost of the title.

## Measures

- Inner circle named, each with a logged conversation in the last fourteen days and a meeting in the last ninety.
- For each inner-circle friend, you can state their current biggest problem. Yes or no, per name.
- Standing annual event held for at least three consecutive years.
- At least one friendship formed in the last three years that is now in the inner or wider ring.

## Canon

- **Vital Friends**, Tom Rath — research on why a small number of deep friendships drives wellbeing more than a large network of weak ones.
- **How to Win Friends and Influence People**, Dale Carnegie — the classic case for initiative and generosity as the mechanics of being someone people want to keep close.
- **The Village Effect**, Susan Pinker — the evidence that face-to-face friendship is a measurable determinant of health, not a nice-to-have.
- **Together**, Vivek H. Murthy — a former U.S. Surgeon General's account of loneliness as a public-health problem and close friendship as the antidote.
