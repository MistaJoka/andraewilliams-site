## What it looks like

The partnership is the primary alliance of the savage's life, and it is treated like one. It gets first claim on time, attention, and honesty — before work, before friends, before the phone. Not because a rule says so, but because the savage chose this person and chooses them again every day in conduct.

It looks like full disclosure. There is nothing your partner would be shocked to learn about your money, your health, your past, or your intentions. Hard conversations happen the day they become necessary, not the month after. Disagreements are argued to resolution with the same respect you would show a colleague you admire — no contempt, no scorekeeping, no raised voice as a tactic.

It looks like logistics handled without a ledger. The savage knows the state of the household, the calendar, the money, and their partner's week, and carries at least half of the mental load without being handed it. Affection is daily and physical. A deliberate, device-free hour together exists most days, and a proper date — planned, not defaulted into — exists every week.

## The gap

The gap looks like a marriage run on autopilot — two people co-managing a calendar, affectionate on anniversaries, silent at dinner. It looks like grievances stored instead of raised, then detonated in a fight about the dishwasher. It looks like a partner who finds out about the debt, the job offer, or the diagnosis after someone else does.

## How to get there

- Raise every grievance within 48 hours or let it go permanently. No third option.
- Run a weekly check-in — thirty minutes, calendar, money, what each of you needs from the other next week. Same slot every week.
- Plan one date per week and own the planning at least half the time. A plan is a time, a place, and a reservation.
- Learn what your partner counts as being loved and deliver it in their currency, not yours. Ask directly if you do not know.
- Protect a device-free window every evening. Phones in another room.

## Measures

- Date nights in the last eight weeks: at least seven, with dates written down.
- Weekly check-in held at least 45 of the last 52 weeks.
- Zero secrets your partner would consider material: money, health, contact with exes, career moves.
- Your partner, asked privately, names you as the person who shows up for them most. Ask them; record the answer and the date.
- Household task audit done within the last ninety days, split within 60/40 either way.
