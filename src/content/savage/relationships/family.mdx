## What it looks like

Family is the one set of relationships the savage did not choose and holds to a standard anyway. It looks like being the person the family calls first when something breaks — the hospital, the eviction, the death — because you have demonstrated, repeatedly, that you answer and you act.

It looks like contact as routine. Parents hear from you weekly, in voice or in person, not in reactions to a group chat. Siblings know what is actually happening in your life and you know theirs. Children, if you have them, get undivided daily attention and a parent who knows their teacher's name, their current fear, and what they are reading. Grandparents and elders are visited while they are alive to be visited.

It looks like knowing the facts. Where your parents' documents are. Who holds power of attorney. What medications they take. The savage has had the uncomfortable conversations about ageing, money, and death while everyone is healthy enough to have them calmly.

## The gap

The gap looks like calling your mother on her birthday and Mother's Day and that is the list. It looks like finding out a sibling moved cities from a photo. It looks like being the relative who "means to visit" and arrives for the funeral. It looks like a parent's decline handled by one sibling alone while the others send thoughts.

## How to get there

- Put a recurring weekly call to each parent on the calendar. Treat it like a meeting you cannot move.
- Hold one full family gathering per year that you organise or co-organise. Date set six months out.
- Have the estate, health, and wishes conversation with each parent before they are 70 or within the year if they already are. Write down where the documents live.
- Give each child fifteen minutes of one-on-one, device-free attention every day. Know one specific thing about their day.
- Visit elders in person at least quarterly. A visit is a day, not a drop-in.

## Measures

- Parents: a voice or in-person conversation logged every week for the last twelve weeks.
- Siblings: you can state each one's current job, city, relationship status, and biggest current problem without checking.
- Elder affairs: location of wills, directives, and medication lists known and written down. Yes or no.
- Family events attended in the last twelve months: every one that mattered, with a list of the ones missed and why.
- Children: fifteen daily minutes delivered at least six days of every seven. Log it for a month.

## Canon

- **Being Mortal**, Atul Gawande — why the estate, health, and end-of-life conversations have to happen while everyone is well enough to have them calmly.
- **Family Evaluation**, Michael Kerr & Murray Bowen — the family systems theory behind why relatives you did not choose still shape you, and how to relate to them on purpose.
- **How to Talk So Kids Will Listen & Listen So Kids Will Talk**, Adele Faber & Elaine Mazlish — the concrete mechanics of undivided daily attention with children that actually lands.
- **Adult Children of Emotionally Immature Parents**, Lindsay C. Gibson — how inherited family patterns get repeated or broken, and what holding a standard with relatives actually requires.
