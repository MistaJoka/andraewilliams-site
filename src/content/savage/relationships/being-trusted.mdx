## What it looks like

Trust is the asset everything else in this domain draws on, and the savage treats it as non-renewable. It looks like a word that is bond. When you say you will be there at seven, you are there at 6:55. When you say you will send it by Friday, it arrives Thursday. When you cannot deliver, the other person hears it from you, early, with a new date — not from silence.

It looks like confidences kept without exception. What a friend tells you about their marriage, what a colleague tells you about their health, what a mentee tells you about their fear — none of it is ever repeated, referenced, hinted at, or used. People tell the savage things because the savage has never once been the source of a leak.

It looks like being the designated person. The emergency contact. The executor. The one with the spare key and the password to the safe. The one a friend's partner calls when they cannot reach the friend.

## The gap

The gap looks like "running ten minutes late" as a personality. It looks like the friend everyone loves but nobody asks to hold the ring. It looks like "I'm not supposed to say anything, but—". It looks like being trusted with a secret and sharing it with your partner as though that does not count. It looks like nobody in your life having named you on a form.

## How to get there

- Under-commit. Say no to anything you are not certain to deliver; a clean no is worth more than a hedged yes.
- Move every commitment into a system the moment it is made — calendar or task list, with the person's name on it. Memory is not a system.
- Renegotiate early. The moment a deadline is at risk, tell the person, with a new date. Never let them discover it.
- Build a hard rule for confidences: repeated to no one, including your partner, unless someone is in danger. Decide the rule once so it is never decided under pressure.
- Arrive early by default. Ten minutes. Every time.

## Measures

- Commitments missed without advance notice in the last ninety days: zero. Log every commitment for a month to check.
- Late arrivals in the last thirty days: count them. The ceiling is zero.
- Number of people who have you listed as emergency contact, executor, guardian, or keyholder: at least three.
- Confidences disclosed to anyone, in any form, in the last year: zero. Audit honestly.

## Canon

- **The Speed of Trust**, Stephen M. R. Covey — trust framed as a measurable economic asset, and the specific behaviours that build or spend it.
- **Trust: The Social Virtues and the Creation of Prosperity**, Francis Fukuyama — trust as the foundation of functioning relationships and institutions alike.
- **Credibility**, James M. Kouzes & Barry Z. Posner — the research on what makes someone believed and followed, independent of authority or title.
- **Extreme Ownership**, Jocko Willink & Leif Babin — reliability and accountability as non-negotiable, with no excuse that survives contact with the standard.
