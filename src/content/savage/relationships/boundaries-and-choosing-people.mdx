## What it looks like

The creed says chosen people. Choosing is the active verb. The savage decides who gets time, trust, and access, and revisits that decision instead of letting proximity, history, or guilt make it. Every close relationship is one the savage would start today if it did not already exist.

It looks like boundaries stated plainly and once. "I don't lend money to family" or "I don't discuss my marriage at work" or "Don't call me after ten unless someone is in hospital" — said in a level voice, without apology, and then enforced without a second speech. A boundary that is not enforced is a preference.

It looks like exits handled cleanly. The friend who only calls to take, the relative whose every visit costs a week of peace, the colleague who trades on your name — the savage drops the temperature deliberately, and where it is warranted, ends it in a direct conversation rather than a slow ghost. Family is not exempt; family is held to a standard too, with a higher bar for the exit and an exit nonetheless.

## The gap

The gap looks like a calendar full of people you would not pick. It looks like resentment as a substitute for a conversation. It looks like lending the money again. It looks like a boundary announced five times and enforced never. It looks like being unable to name a single relationship you have ended on purpose.

## How to get there

- Audit annually. List every person who got more than ten hours of your time last year. Mark each one: invest, maintain, reduce, end. Act on the marks within the quarter.
- Write your standing boundaries down — five to ten of them — so they are decided once and stated the same way every time.
- Enforce on the first breach. Name it, restate the boundary, apply the consequence. No third warning.
- End relationships in person or by voice, in plain words, when the relationship was ever close. Silence is for the ones that never were.
- Say no in one sentence. Add no reason unless asked.

## Measures

- Relationship audit completed and dated within the last twelve months, with every name marked.
- Every "reduce" or "end" from the last audit acted on. Count the ones still unchanged.
- Standing boundaries written down. Yes or no.
- Boundary breaches in the last ninety days where you did not enforce on the first occurrence: zero.
- At least one relationship deliberately ended in your adult life, and you can state why in one sentence.

## Canon

- **Boundaries**, Henry Cloud & John Townsend — the foundational case for deciding, stating, and enforcing limits as an act of respect, not selfishness.
- **Necessary Endings**, Henry Cloud — when and how to end what should end, in business and in relationships, before it costs more than it gives.
- **When I Say No, I Feel Guilty**, Manuel J. Smith — the assertiveness classic on stating a boundary in one sentence and holding it without a justification.
- **The Dance of Anger**, Harriet Lerner — the relational patterns that keep boundaries unenforced, and what changing the pattern actually requires.
