## What it looks like

Runway is the number that decides whether money is a tool or a threat. The savage holds twelve months of fixed costs in cash — instantly accessible, boring, never invested — and touches it only for an actual emergency. A broken car is not an emergency; it is a line in the maintenance fund. Job loss, a medical event, a family member who needs a flight tonight: that is what the reserve is for.

The savings rate at the ceiling is fifty percent of net income, achieved by automation rather than heroism. Money leaves the account the day it arrives, split by rule into reserve, investments, sinking funds, and the amount allowed to be spent. What remains in checking is what can be spent, so no willpower is required.

Sinking funds exist for every large, predictable expense — vehicle replacement, home repairs, travel, annual premiums, taxes. Nothing predictable is ever a surprise, and nothing predictable ever goes on credit. The savage knows the runway number (see MNY·01) and watches it only to see it grow.

## The gap

The gap looks like savings that happen if anything is left at month end, which it never is. It looks like a one-month emergency fund raided for a holiday. It looks like a known, predictable car repair becoming a card balance because the money was never set aside. It looks like a five percent savings rate described as "pretty good."

## How to get there

- Open a separate reserve account at a different institution from daily banking, with no card attached. Friction is the feature.
- Set the automatic transfer for the day after payday. Raise it two percentage points every quarter until it reaches fifty.
- Fill the reserve to three months, then six, then twelve. Until it is at three, nothing else in this domain matters except debt above ten percent (see MNY·05).
- Create a sinking fund for every expense over five hundred that arrives less than monthly. Divide by months until due; automate that too.
- Every income increase goes to the rate, not the lifestyle, until the rate is at ceiling (see MNY·02).
- Define "emergency" in writing, now, while calm. Read it before every withdrawal.

## Measures

- Cash reserve divided by monthly fixed costs: twelve or above.
- Savings rate over the trailing twelve months: fifty percent or above.
- Automated transfers exist for reserve, investments, and every sinking fund; zero skipped in the last year.
- Zero reserve withdrawals in the last twenty-four months outside the written definition.
- Every expense over five hundred in the last year was paid from a fund that existed before the expense.

## Canon

- **Your Money or Your Life**, Vicki Robin & Joe Dominguez — reframes spending against life energy, the root argument for a radical savings rate.
- **The Simple Path to Wealth**, JL Collins — the case for a brutal savings rate and cash held apart from anything invested.
- **The Automatic Millionaire**, David Bach — the mechanics of paying yourself first so the rate survives without willpower.
