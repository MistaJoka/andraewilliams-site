## What it looks like

Money cannot be under command if it is not measured. The savage keeps one ledger — spreadsheet, ledger file, app, the tool does not matter — holding every account, every balance, every recurring obligation, and a dated net-worth line for every month going back years.

Ask the savage what they are worth and the answer comes in under three seconds, to the nearest thousand. Ask what the household spends in a month and it comes just as fast, split into fixed and variable. Ask what happens if income stops tomorrow and the answer is a number of months, not a shrug.

Reconciliation is a ritual, not a panic. On a fixed day each month, every account is pulled, every transaction categorised, the net-worth line written, and cash flow compared to the last twelve months. Tax liability is estimated quarterly, not discovered at filing. Every subscription is listed by name, amount, and renewal date. The whole thing takes under an hour, and the savage has not skipped a month in years.

## The gap

The gap looks like checking the balance before a purchase to see if it will clear. It looks like "roughly" as the answer to every money question. It looks like three banking apps, none showing the whole picture, and a card statement paid without being read. It looks like a tax bill that arrives as news, and a net worth never once written down because writing it down would make it real.

## How to get there

- Build the ledger this week. One sheet: every asset, every liability, one net-worth total. Date it.
- Pull twelve months of statements and categorise every transaction into no more than fifteen buckets. The number that falls out is your real burn; believe it over your estimate.
- Set a reconciliation day — the first, the fifteenth, payday — as a recurring calendar block. Miss it for nothing.
- Split spending into fixed and variable. Cash on hand divided by fixed is your runway in months (see MNY·03).
- Cancel every subscription you could not name unprompted.
- Keep a one-page "if I am hit by a bus" sheet: every account, institution, and where access lives (see MNY·06).

## Measures

- Net worth stated from memory, then checked against the ledger: within five percent.
- Monthly reconciliation log has no gap longer than forty-five days in the last two years.
- Monthly burn, fixed and variable, known to the nearest hundred.
- Every recurring charge listed by name, amount, and renewal date; zero unidentified transactions in the last ninety days.
- Quarterly tax estimate dated within the last ninety days.
