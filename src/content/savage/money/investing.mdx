## What it looks like

The savage's capital is deployed, not parked. Every dollar above the reserve and sinking funds is working, according to a one-page investment policy written in calm conditions: target allocation, rebalancing rule, contribution schedule, and the conditions under which the plan changes — which do not include fear, news, or a friend's tip.

The principles are unglamorous because the evidence is. Broad diversification across asset classes and geographies. Low fees — total expense under a quarter of a percent, every basis point known. Tax-advantaged accounts filled to their limits before taxable ones are touched. Contributions automated on a fixed day regardless of price. Rebalancing on a calendar or a threshold, never a feeling. A horizon in decades, so a forty percent drawdown is expected, priced in, and met with the next scheduled contribution rather than a sale.

Speculation, if it exists, is ring-fenced: a fixed small percentage that can go to zero without changing a plan. Concentrated bets — employer stock, a single property — are sized and unwound on a schedule. The savage can explain every holding in one sentence and the whole portfolio in one minute.

## The gap

The gap looks like cash sitting in checking for three years because investing felt like a decision. It looks like a portfolio of whatever was popular the year each account was opened. It looks like selling in the crash and buying back at the top, twice. It looks like fees nobody has calculated, forty percent in one company's stock because selling was effort, and a plan that lives in someone's head and changes with their mood.

## How to get there

- Write the investment policy this month. One page: allocation, contribution, rebalance rule, speculation cap, change conditions. Sign and date it.
- Calculate total fee drag across every account. Move anything above a quarter of a percent unless a written reason says it earns the difference.
- Fill tax-advantaged space first, every year, to the limit.
- Automate contributions on payday. Never time them.
- Rebalance annually, or when any asset class drifts five points from target, and at no other time.
- Unwind concentrated positions to under ten percent of net worth on a written timeline.
- Read the portfolio no more than monthly. The policy does the watching.

## Measures

- Investment policy exists, one page, dated; every holding complies with it.
- Weighted total expense ratio across all accounts: under 0.25 percent, calculated within the last year.
- Tax-advantaged accounts at their annual limit for each of the last three years.
- No single position above ten percent of net worth, or a dated unwind schedule exists.
- Zero unscheduled sales in the last five years.
- No missed scheduled contribution in the last twenty-four months.
