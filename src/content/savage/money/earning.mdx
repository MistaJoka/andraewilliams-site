## What it looks like

The savage earns in multiples. Gross income is at least three times the cost of living, and the gap is not closed by spending more — it is the raw material for everything else in this domain. Savings rate, runway, investing, and generosity all live in that gap.

Income comes from leverage, not only hours. The savage is paid for judgement, ownership, or output that scales: equity, a business, products that sell while they sleep, or a skill rare enough that the market bids for it. Trading one hour for one unit of pay is where everyone starts; it is not where the savage stays.

No single source can end the income. There are at least two streams, and the second covers fixed costs on its own. Losing the primary is a bad quarter, not a catastrophe. The savage knows their market rate within ten percent, renegotiates on a schedule, and has walked away from a number before. Earning power gets the same treatment as strength: deliberate weekly investment, with a log.

## The gap

The gap looks like a raise asked for once, five years ago. It looks like not knowing what peers are paid. It looks like one employer, one paycheque, and a quiet terror around every reorganisation. It looks like a side project that has been "almost ready" for three years, and income that rose with lifestyle in lockstep so the multiple never moved.

## How to get there

- Find your market rate this month: three data points from postings, peers, or recruiters. If you are below it, open the negotiation within thirty days.
- Write down the income multiple — gross income divided by annual spend — and the date. Move it one turn every two years until it passes three.
- Pick one second stream and give it a fixed weekly block until it produces its first paid unit. Then make it cover one fixed cost, then all of them.
- Buy leverage: ownership, equity, products, or a skill in the top one percent of its field. Hourly work has a ceiling; know where yours is.
- One substantive conversation per week with someone who could hire, refer, or partner with you.
- Bank the difference every time income rises (see MNY·03). Lifestyle moves on your schedule, not the raise's.

## Measures

- Gross income divided by annual spend: three or above.
- Income streams that independently cover fixed costs: at least one beyond the primary.
- Market rate known within ten percent, sourced and dated within the last twelve months.
- Last compensation renegotiation dated within the last eighteen months.
- Weekly skill-investment block logged for forty of the last fifty-two weeks.
