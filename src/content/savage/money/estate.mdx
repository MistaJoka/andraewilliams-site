## What it looks like

Death is a logistics problem, and the savage has already solved it. Every account that allows a named beneficiary has one, current, correctly spelled, and that beneficiary designation is understood to override the will — so the will is checked against the beneficiaries, not the other way round. Nothing defaults to an ex-partner or a long-closed employer because nobody thought to update it.

A will exists and is current. Where net worth or property makes probate expensive or slow, a revocable living trust is funded — assets actually retitled into it, not just a document sitting unused in a drawer. Transfer-on-death and payable-on-death designations are used wherever they are available, so as much of the estate as possible bypasses probate entirely. Anyone with minor children has named guardians in writing, and those guardians agreed to it before it was written down.

An executor is named and briefed — not surprised at a funeral. They know where the original documents are, and they have emergency access to whatever unlocks everything else: password manager, safe deposit box, the one-page sheet from MNY·06 taken to full estate-plan depth — complete asset inventory, every institution, every contact, every account, every wish, in one place a grieving person can actually follow. The whole plan is reviewed on the same fixed date as the annual risk review (see MNY·06), and again within thirty days of marriage, divorce, a birth, a death in the family, or a major purchase.

## The gap

The gap looks like a will drafted the year the first child was born and never touched since, while the family it describes has completely changed. It looks like a 401(k) still naming an ex-spouse, or a life insurance policy naming a parent who died five years ago. It looks like no guardian named for the kids, so a court decides. It looks like an executor who finds out they are the executor from the lawyer, and a password to everything that dies with the person who knew it.

## How to get there

- Pull every account that allows a beneficiary designation. Check each one against the current, actual wishes. Fix every wrong or missing name this week.
- Execute or update the will with an estate attorney. If net worth or property warrants it, fund a revocable living trust — retitle the assets, don't just sign the paperwork.
- Name guardians for every minor child in writing. Ask them directly before naming them.
- Name an executor. Tell them, in person, where the documents are and how to reach the professionals involved.
- Add transfer-on-death or payable-on-death designations to every account and asset that allows them.
- Write the estate-plan-grade version of the one-page sheet (see MNY·06): full asset inventory, every institution, every contact, location of every original document, emergency access to the password manager.
- Put the review on the same calendar date as the annual risk review (see MNY·06). Re-run it within thirty days of any major life event.

## Measures

- Every account with a beneficiary option has a current, correctly named beneficiary, checked within the last twelve months.
- Will executed and current; trust funded and titled correctly if one exists.
- Guardians for every minor child named in writing, with their documented agreement.
- Executor named, briefed, and holding emergency access to documents and passwords.
- Zero assets that would default to probate where a transfer-on-death or payable-on-death designation was available instead.
- Estate plan reviewed within the last twelve months, or within thirty days of the most recent major life event, whichever is more recent.

## Canon

- **Plan Your Estate**, Denis Clifford (Nolo) — the standing general reference for what a complete estate plan actually contains, will through trust.
- **Beyond the Grave**, Gerald M. Condon & Jeffrey L. Condon — the case for a plan specific enough that it doesn't quietly start a family fight.
- **8 Ways to Avoid Probate**, Mary Randolph (Nolo) — the mechanics behind using beneficiary and transfer-on-death designations to bypass probate.
- **Die With Zero**, Bill Perkins — the argument for transferring wealth deliberately while alive, not only by default at death.
