## What it looks like

Debt under command is a calculation, not a condition. The savage carries no consumer debt of any kind: no revolving card balance, no financed furniture, no car loan, no buy-now-pay-later. Cards are paid in full on every statement without exception, used for the float and the protections, never for the credit.

What debt remains is deliberate. A mortgage at a rate below the expected return of invested capital, on a property that fits well inside the income. Perhaps a business loan with a written return case. Each has a purpose written down, a payoff date, and a rate below what the money would earn elsewhere. When that stops being true, the debt goes.

Every liability is on the ledger (see MNY·01) with balance, rate, minimum, and payoff date. The savage could clear all non-mortgage debt tomorrow from cash without touching the reserve, and knows it. The credit profile is excellent — not because credit will be used, but because the option is cheap to keep and expensive to rebuild. The savage has never co-signed, never loaned money they needed back, and never carried a balance "just this month."

## The gap

The gap looks like a card balance that rolls over every month, described as "manageable." It looks like a seven-year loan on a car worth less than the balance. It looks like knowing the minimum but not the rate, debt taken on to keep an appearance up, and a monthly total of all payments never once added because the number would be unbearable.

## How to get there

- List every debt tonight: balance, rate, minimum, payoff date. Total the monthly payments. Look at the number.
- No new consumer debt from today. Freeze the cards if that is what it takes.
- Attack anything above ten percent before any investing beyond an employer match (see MNY·04). Highest rate first, every spare dollar, until it is gone.
- Below ten percent, run the arithmetic — pay down or invest, by rate versus expected return — decide once in writing, and follow it.
- Refinance deliberate debt whenever the rate can drop a point or more. Check annually.
- Pull the credit report from every bureau yearly. Dispute every error.
- Write a one-line debt policy — what you will borrow for and what you never will — and keep it with the investment policy.

## Measures

- Consumer debt balance: zero, for the last twenty-four months.
- Every card statement in the last twenty-four months paid in full, on time.
- Each remaining liability has a written purpose, a payoff date, and a rate below the portfolio's expected return.
- Cash on hand, excluding the reserve, exceeds all non-mortgage debt.
- Credit report pulled from every bureau within the last twelve months, zero unresolved errors.
- Total debt service under fifteen percent of gross income.

## Canon

- **The Total Money Makeover**, Dave Ramsey — the uncompromising case for carrying zero consumer debt.
- **Your Credit Score**, Liz Pulliam Weston — managing the credit profile as a cheap-to-keep tool, not a leash.
- **Rich Dad Poor Dad**, Robert Kiyosaki — the good-debt/bad-debt distinction this standard formalizes with a rate test.
