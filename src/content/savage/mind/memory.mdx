## What it looks like

The savage does not forget what mattered. Names are held from the first introduction. Numbers that run the savage's own life — account figures, dates, doses, dimensions — are known cold. Material studied a year ago can be reproduced, not just recognised.

This is not a gift. It is a system with two halves. The first is internal: retrieval practice instead of rereading, spacing instead of cramming, elaboration — explaining the thing in your own words and tying it to what you already hold. The second is external: a single, searchable store of notes that the savage actually reads, where anything worth keeping is written down in a form that will still make sense in ten years.

The ceiling reads: a spaced-repetition deck reviewed daily with a true streak, every serious book or course finished with written notes in the savage's own words, and a personal knowledge base that can answer "what did I conclude about this" for any significant topic of the last decade in under a minute.

## The gap

The gap looks like rereading highlights and feeling informed. It looks like "I read that somewhere." It looks like meeting someone for the third time and reaching for their name. It looks like ten thousand notes in four apps, none of which have been opened since they were written.

## How to get there

- Test, do not reread. After any study session, close the source and write what you can recall. Check. Fix the gaps.
- Run spaced repetition daily — twenty minutes is enough. Put in only what you have decided to keep forever.
- Take notes in your own sentences, never copied. A note that cannot be understood without the source is not a note.
- One system, one place, searchable, backed up. Migrate or delete everything else.
- Names: repeat it on introduction, use it twice in the conversation, write it down within the hour with one distinguishing fact.
- Review the knowledge base on a schedule — weekly for recent notes, quarterly for the archive — and prune what no longer earns its place.

## Measures

- Spaced-repetition streak is unbroken for the last ninety days, and the deck contains a thousand or more active cards.
- Every book or course completed in the last year has a dated note file in your own words.
- Closed-book test: write a one-page summary of something studied twelve months ago; compare it to your notes. It holds.
- Name recall: in the last month, zero instances of failing to name someone met more than once.
