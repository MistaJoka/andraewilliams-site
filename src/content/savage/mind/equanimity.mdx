## What it looks like

Equanimity is not suppression and it is not indifference. The savage feels everything — anger, fear, grief, elation — at full strength and acts on none of it until it has been examined. The distance between stimulus and response is a trained space, and the savage lives in it.

Under pressure the savage gets quieter, not louder. Bad news is received with a question, not a reaction. When everyone else is running hot, the savage's voice is the one the room steadies on, and that is deliberate.

Internally the standard is honest accounting. The savage knows which triggers bite — criticism, being ignored, loss of control, a certain tone from a certain person — and has a practice for each. Recovery from a genuine blow is measured in hours, not weeks. Mood does not dictate whether the work gets done (see MND·03).

The ceiling reads: a daily practice that builds the capacity — meditation, breathwork, reflective writing, or cold exposure, twenty minutes minimum — held for years. Zero decisions of consequence made while hot. Zero regrets about something said in the last year.

## The gap

The gap looks like a reply sent at midnight and a morning spent explaining it. It looks like being managed by the people around you because they have learned what sets you off. It looks like a week lost to a comment. It looks like calm that is actually avoidance — nothing felt, nothing faced, and a bill arriving later with interest.

## How to get there

- Establish the daily practice and hold it for ninety days before evaluating it. Twenty minutes, same time, no exceptions.
- Name the triggers in writing. For each, write the body signal that precedes the reaction and the action you take when you notice it.
- Institute the delay. Anything written in anger waits until the next morning. Anything decided in fear waits twenty-four hours.
- Journal the hard days: what happened, what you felt, what you did, what you would do at the ceiling. Patterns appear within a month.
- Seek voluntary hardship — cold, fasting, hard conditioning, difficult conversations — to keep the threshold high (see BDY·02).

## Measures

- Practice log: twenty or more minutes daily for the last ninety days, with no more than three missed days.
- Written trigger list exists, has been updated in the last six months, and names a response for each entry.
- Zero messages, decisions, or purchases in the last ninety days that you would reverse if you could. Check the sent folder.
- Ask three people who see you under pressure to rate your composure out of ten. Median is eight or higher.
