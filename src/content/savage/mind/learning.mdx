## What it looks like

Learning is the strength training of the Mind domain. The savage does not "stay curious"; the savage runs a program. At any moment there is one named hard thing under active study — a language, a discipline, an instrument, a body of mathematics — with a start date, a defined level of competence that counts as done, and a weekly hour count that is actually met.

The work is deliberate practice, not exposure. It sits at the edge of current ability, produces errors, and the errors get corrected the same session. Reading about a skill is preparation; the skill is built by doing it badly in front of feedback until it is done well. The savage seeks out the coach, the test, the review, the public attempt — whatever makes the gap visible.

The ceiling reads: a minimum of five focused hours per week on the current hard thing, one new domain taken to demonstrable working competence every twelve to eighteen months, and a written record of what was learned that a stranger could follow.

## The gap

The gap looks like a shelf of half-read books and a queue of saved courses. It looks like "I've been meaning to." It looks like ten years of the same skill set, renamed. It looks like consuming explanations and mistaking the feeling of understanding for the ability to perform.

## How to get there

- Name the current hard thing, the competence bar, and the deadline. Write it where it will be seen daily.
- Block the hours in the calendar before the week starts. Five hours minimum; the first one before anything optional.
- Practice at the edge: choose exercises that fail about a third of the time. Below that the work is review; above it the work is noise.
- Get feedback faster than you get comfortable. A tutor, a test suite, a recording, a rubric, a public audience.
- Finish. Reach the stated bar or kill the project in writing with the reason. No silent abandonment.
- Keep a learning log: date, hours, what was attempted, what broke, what was fixed.

## Measures

- One named hard thing in progress, with bar and deadline written down and dated.
- Learning log shows five or more focused hours every week for the last twelve weeks, with no gap longer than one week.
- At least one skill taken from zero to demonstrable competence in the last eighteen months, with evidence a stranger could verify.
- Every abandoned project in the last three years has a dated kill note.

## Canon

- **Peak: Secrets from the New Science of Expertise**, Anders Ericsson & Robert Pool — the deliberate practice research this entire standard is built on.
- **Ultralearning**, Scott Young — the playbook for structuring an intense, self-directed learning project with a bar and a deadline.
- **Make It Stick**, Peter C. Brown, Henry L. Roediger III & Mark A. McDaniel — the evidence that testing and struggle beat rereading and comfort.
- **Mindset**, Carol S. Dweck — why treating ability as trainable is a precondition for doing any of this.
