## What it looks like

Deep work is the conditioning of the Mind domain. The savage can sit down, open a single hard problem, and stay on it for ninety minutes to three hours without checking anything.

The day is structured around this. The deep block is scheduled first, before mail, before messages, before anyone else's agenda. Phones, notifications, and open tabs are not in the room. Interruptions are handled in batches at set times. People who need the savage know when to expect a reply and are not surprised by silence.

The ceiling reads: three to four hours of logged deep work on most working days, in blocks of at least ninety minutes, with the first block starting within an hour of beginning work. Total screen-driven distraction — feeds, inboxes, chat — is under an hour a day and occurs at chosen times. The savage can enter the state within ten minutes of sitting down.

## The gap

The gap looks like a day of being busy that produced nothing you could point to. It looks like checking messages forty times before noon and calling the result "communication." It looks like needing a deadline to concentrate. It looks like a twenty-minute attention span dressed up as multitasking. It looks like a phone on the desk, face down, buzzing.

## How to get there

- Block the deep hours in the calendar as appointments. Defend them like a flight.
- Remove the choice: phone in another room, notifications off at the OS level, blocker on during blocks, one application open.
- Train the duration. Start at whatever you can honestly hold — forty minutes — and add ten minutes a week until ninety is easy and three hours is possible.
- Define the task before the block starts. A block with no stated target becomes browsing.
- Batch shallow work into two fixed windows a day. Reply then; nowhere else.
- Protect the substrate: sleep, conditioning, and daylight are not optional (see BDY·04, BDY·02).

## Measures

- Deep-work log shows the daily hours for the last thirty days; median on working days is three or more.
- Longest single uninterrupted block in the last week is ninety minutes or more.
- Device screen-time report shows under sixty minutes per day on feeds, inboxes, and chat, averaged over the last week.
- Notifications are off for everything except named humans and emergencies. Check the settings now.

## Canon

- **Deep Work**, Cal Newport — the case for undistracted concentration as an economically and personally decisive skill.
- **Digital Minimalism**, Cal Newport — the philosophy behind stripping the environment of what fragments attention.
- **Flow**, Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi — the psychology of the state this standard is training you to enter on demand.
- **Indistractable**, Nir Eyal — a practical model for tracing distraction to its internal triggers and removing them.
