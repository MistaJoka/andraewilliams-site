## What it looks like

The mind is built from what it is fed. The savage chooses every input the way an athlete chooses every meal, and the standard is the same: dense, real, and earned.

The reading is serious. Primary sources over summaries. Books over articles. Textbooks, histories, and the foundational works of a field over the commentary on them. A portion of what is read is deliberately difficult — older, longer, or from a discipline the savage has not mastered — because easy reading builds nothing.

The ceiling reads: an hour or more of book reading every day, forty or more books a year of which at least a third are hard, and a written note on each one (see MND·04). Reading is active: margins marked, arguments tested, sources checked when a claim is load-bearing.

The other side of the diet is refusal. News is consumed on a schedule or not at all — a weekly digest is enough for anyone who does not trade on it. Social feeds are either deleted or caged to a timed window. Nothing algorithmic chooses what the savage thinks about today.

## The gap

The gap looks like three hours a day of scrolling and "no time to read." It looks like opinions about every book of the year and the completion of none. It looks like knowing today's outrage in detail and the history behind it not at all. It looks like reading only what confirms, only what is new, only what is short.

## How to get there

- Set the daily hour and put it at a fixed time — first thing or last thing, when nothing competes.
- Keep a reading list with a ratio: of every three books, one hard, one from outside your field, one of your choosing.
- Read with a pen. Finish with a one-page note in your own words before the next book is opened.
- Delete the feed apps from the phone. If a feed must remain, set a timer that ends it at fifteen minutes and obey the timer.
- Replace news with one weekly digest from a source you chose for quality.
- Audit the diet monthly: list every recurring input and cut anything that has not changed a decision or taught you something in ninety days.

## Measures

- Reading log shows a completed book with date and note at least every nine days over the last year.
- At least a third of those books would be called hard by a literate stranger.
- Phone screen-time report: social and news combined under fifteen minutes per day, averaged over the last week.
- The monthly input audit exists and is dated within the last thirty-one days.

## Canon

- **How to Read a Book**, Mortimer J. Adler & Charles Van Doren — the method for reading actively at increasing levels of demand, still the reference for the practice.
- **The Shallows**, Nicholas Carr — the case against the feed-driven attention economy and what it costs deep reading specifically.
- **A Mind for Numbers**, Barbara Oakley — practical grounding in how difficult, unfamiliar material actually gets learned by reading and rereading with structure.
- **Amusing Ourselves to Death**, Neil Postman — the argument for why the form of the medium, not just its content, shapes what a mind can hold.
