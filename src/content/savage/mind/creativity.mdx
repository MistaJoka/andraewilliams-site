## What it looks like

Creativity is a discipline, not a mood. The savage produces on a schedule, keeps the output, and ships a portion of it. Ideas are captured the moment they arrive — every one, without judgement — and reviewed on a cadence where most are killed and a few are built. The inventory is the asset.

Problem solving has a method and it is run every time. Define the problem in one sentence. State what a solved problem looks like. Break it down to its parts. Generate options — at least ten, including three that are obviously wrong, because they unstick the good ones. Pick by explicit criteria. Act on the smallest test that could prove the approach wrong, and iterate on what comes back.

The ceiling reads: something made every day — a sketch, a paragraph, a prototype, a piece of code, a melody — and one finished work shipped to an audience every quarter. Across domains, because the savage steals structure from one field to solve problems in another, and that is where most original work comes from.

## The gap

The gap looks like waiting for inspiration. It looks like a notebook of ideas never reopened. It looks like solving every problem with the first approach that came to mind, and every problem looking like the last one. It looks like brainstorming sessions where the first idea wins because evaluating in the moment killed the rest.

## How to get there

- Make something daily. Thirty minutes, fixed time, output kept and dated. Quality is not the point for the first year; volume is.
- Capture every idea within sixty seconds, one place, no filtering. Review weekly; promote or kill.
- Write out the method and use it on paper for every hard problem until it is automatic: problem, done-state, parts, ten options, criteria, smallest test.
- Separate generation from judgement. Nothing gets evaluated until the list is full.
- Read and train outside your field on purpose (see MND·01, MND·05). Imported structure is the cheapest originality there is.
- Ship a finished piece every ninety days. Finished means someone outside your head has it.

## Measures

- Daily-making log: entries dated on at least twenty-five of the last thirty days.
- Idea inventory has a hundred or more entries and a weekly review dated within the last seven days.
- Last hard problem you faced has a written worksheet with ten or more options and the chosen criteria.
- Something finished and shipped to an audience in each of the last four quarters, with dates.
