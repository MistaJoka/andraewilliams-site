## What it looks like

Discipline is the mechanism that turns every other standard in this book from an intention into a record. The savage has a small set of daily and weekly non-negotiables — training, the work block, the early start, the review — and they happen whether or not the day cooperates. Not because motivation was found, but because the question of whether to do them was settled once, long ago, and is not reopened each morning.

It is quiet. Nobody is told about the 5:30 alarm. The run in the rain is not photographed. The discipline is visible only in the log and in the results that compound from it: the training history without gaps (BDY·01), the body of work that exists because the hours were put in, the promises kept (CHR·01).

It is also calibrated. The savage knows the difference between resistance and injury, fatigue and illness, and does not confuse hard for stupid. A planned rest day is discipline. An unplanned one is the gap.

## The gap

The gap looks like "I'll start Monday." It looks like streaks that reset every six weeks, a habit tracker with more blank squares than marks, a gym membership as an identity. It looks like needing a mood, a playlist, or a new notebook before the work can begin. It looks like negotiating with yourself at 5:30 and losing, and calling the loss "listening to my body".

## How to get there

- Fix the non-negotiables in writing: no more than five daily, three weekly. Each has a time, a place, and a minimum dose that can be done on the worst day (ten minutes counts; zero does not).
- Decide once. Remove the morning decision entirely — clothes laid out, route planned, first task written the night before.
- Track it. A single line per day, pen or plain text. The log is the only authority on whether you are disciplined; feelings are not consulted.
- Never miss twice. One miss is a data point. Two in a row is a new habit forming. The second day is the one that matters.
- Raise the bar only after ninety days of unbroken execution at the current one.

## Measures

- Written list of non-negotiables exists, unchanged for at least ninety days.
- Daily log shows 95% or better completion of non-negotiables over the last twelve months — that is no more than eighteen misses, none consecutive.
- Longest gap in any non-negotiable over the last year: two days or fewer, excluding planned rest that was scheduled in advance.
- Zero non-negotiables dropped in the last year without a deliberate, dated decision to retire them.
