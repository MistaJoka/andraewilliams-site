## What it looks like

The savage owns outcomes, not efforts. "I worked hard on it" is not a sentence they use. When something they are responsible for fails — a deadline, a relationship, a decision that looked right — the first word out is "I", and the next sentence is the fix. The circumstances, the colleague, the market, the childhood: all may be true, none are offered.

Ownership extends past the formal boundary. If the team missed, the savage missed. If a problem sits between two departments, they pick it up rather than point at it. If a task is handed to them poorly specified, they own the clarification. There is no "not my job" anywhere in their vocabulary.

Bad news travels up fast and from them. A slipping project is flagged the day it slips, with a revised plan, not discovered at the review. Mistakes are disclosed in full the same day they are found, including the ones nobody would have caught. The savage has never been surprised by a failure they caused becoming public, because they made it public first (CHR·01).

## The gap

The gap looks like the passive voice: "mistakes were made", "it fell through the cracks", "the process failed". It looks like a post-mortem where every finding points at a system or someone else. It looks like the slipped deadline announced on the deadline. It looks like a life narrated as a series of things that happened to you, and a person who is always, somehow, the victim of their own decisions.

## How to get there

- Ban the excuse sentence. For every failure, write one line that starts with "I" and names the decision or omission. If you cannot write it, you do not yet understand the failure.
- Report every slip within one working day of knowing, with three things: what slipped, what it now costs, what you will do. Never let the deadline be the messenger.
- Run a personal post-mortem on every significant failure within seven days: cause, your contribution, one concrete change. Keep them in a file. Re-read the file quarterly.
- Take the orphaned problems. When something falls between people, say "I'll own that" before anyone has to ask.
- Ask for the hard feedback directly and quarterly: "What am I doing that you have stopped telling me about?" Then act on it without defence.

## Measures

- Post-mortem file exists with at least one entry per quarter; every entry names your own contribution first.
- Zero failures in the last year that someone else reported before you did.
- Last three slips: each disclosed within one working day, with a revised plan attached. Dates on record.
- Ask the people you work with: "When something goes wrong, does this person own it or explain it?" The answer is "owns it", unanimously.
