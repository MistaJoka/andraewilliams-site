## What it looks like

Integrity is the keystone of the Character domain. Every other standard here is a special case of it. The savage treats a spoken commitment as binding in exactly the way most people treat a signed one — and says less, because of it. "I'll try" does not leave the mouth. Either the answer is yes and it gets done, or the answer is no and it is given on the spot.

Truth is told in full, including the parts that cost something. There is no version of a story for the person who was there and another for the person who was not. Numbers are reported as they are: the revenue, the weight on the bar, the hours actually worked. The savage does not round in their own favour, and does not let an inaccurate compliment stand uncorrected.

When a commitment cannot be kept, the other party hears it from you first, early, with a date for the replacement. They never discover it by the deadline passing. Small promises are kept with the same weight as large ones — the 9:00 call happens at 9:00, the borrowed book comes back — because people judge your word on the small ones.

## The gap

The gap looks like reliability with an asterisk. It looks like "I meant to", "something came up", and the quiet hope that the other person forgot. It looks like a story that drifts with each retelling, the white lie that keeps a dinner pleasant, the invoice that is technically accurate and practically misleading. It looks like being honest when honesty is free and flexible when it is not.

## How to get there

- Keep a dated log of every commitment made, with the date it was kept or renegotiated. Review it weekly. Nothing ages past its date without a conversation.
- Cut the vocabulary of escape: no "try", "hopefully", "should be able to". Replace with a date or a no.
- Say no more. The fastest way to keep every promise is to make fewer of them, deliberately.
- Correct the record within twenty-four hours of discovering you were wrong, misquoted, or over-credited — unprompted, in the same channel the error travelled.
- Hold one standard of truth for every audience: partner, client, stranger, tax authority. If a statement would not survive all four hearing it, do not make it.

## Measures

- Commitment log exists; zero entries in the last ninety days broken without prior notice to the other party.
- Zero known statements in the last year you would need to amend if the full facts surfaced.
- Every correction of your own error in the last year was initiated by you, not by discovery.
- Ask three people who rely on you: "Has my word ever not held?" The answer is no, with no pause.
