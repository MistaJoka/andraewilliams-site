## What it looks like

Courage in a modern life is rarely physical. It is the conversation you have been avoiding, held this week. It is disagreeing with the most senior person in the room when they are wrong, in the room, not in the corridor afterwards. It is asking for the number you are worth, quitting the job that is slowly killing you, telling a friend the thing they need to hear and will not thank you for.

The savage feels fear in full. The difference is latency: the gap between knowing what is right and doing it is measured in hours, not months. The decision is made on the merits and the fear is carried along as a passenger.

It is also the courage to be seen failing. To ship the work before it is perfect (see the Craft domain), to be the worst person in the class, to say "I don't know" and "I was wrong" out loud. And when it is physical — someone being harassed on a train, a person collapsed on the pavement — the savage is the one who moves toward it while others study their phones.

## The gap

The gap looks like a hard conversation scheduled for "when the time is right". It looks like agreeing in meetings and complaining afterwards, the resignation letter drafted for a year, the feedback softened until it says nothing. It looks like a life arranged so that nothing is ever risked: safe job, safe opinions, safe silence. It looks like calling avoidance "picking your battles" when no battle has ever been picked.

## How to get there

- Keep a list titled "Avoiding". Anything on it for more than seven days gets a date and gets done. The list is reviewed every Sunday.
- Practise the small version daily: send the blunt email, return the wrong order, state the unpopular view in the meeting. Courage is a muscle; load it at low weight, often.
- Decide before the moment. Write down in advance what you will do when a senior person is wrong, when a friend is out of line, when a stranger needs help. Pre-decided acts do not need bravery at the time.
- Separate the fear from the decision. Write the decision as if a braver person were making it for you, then do that.
- Train the physical capacity to intervene (BDY·02) so that stepping forward is an option and not a bluff.

## Measures

- "Avoiding" list exists; no item on it is older than fourteen days.
- Count the hard conversations held in the last ninety days. Fewer than three means something is being dodged.
- Last time you openly disagreed with someone who outranked you, in front of others: dated, within the last month.
- Last time you physically moved toward a situation others moved away from: recalled specifically, and not from a decade ago.

## Canon

- **Courage Is Calling**, Ryan Holiday — a modern, direct treatment of courage as the virtue that makes every other virtue possible, matching this page's ordering logic.
- **Daring Greatly**, Brené Brown — the research case that vulnerability and the willingness to be seen failing are prerequisites for courage, not opposites of it.
- **Feel the Fear and Do It Anyway**, Susan Jeffers — a practical case that fear and correct action are meant to coexist, not that fear resolves first.
- **The War of Art**, Steven Pressfield — names the daily resistance to the hard, necessary thing, and the discipline of acting through it anyway.
