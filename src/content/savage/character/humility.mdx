## What it looks like

Humility is not low self-regard. The savage knows exactly what they are good at and does not pretend otherwise. What they refuse is the need to be seen as right. When the evidence turns, so do they — out loud, quickly, crediting whoever turned it. "You're right, I was wrong" is said in full sentences, in front of the same people who heard the original position.

They are a beginner somewhere at all times. Every year there is a skill at which they are visibly, publicly bad, taking instruction from someone younger, cheaper, or less credentialed. They ask the obvious question in the room instead of nodding. They take notes when a junior explains something. Ranks, titles, and follower counts do not change whom they listen to.

Criticism is sought, not survived. The savage asks for it, reads it twice, and replies with what they will change — never with the reason the critic is mistaken, even when the critic is. Thanks come first; rebuttal, if any, comes days later and in private.

## The gap

The gap looks like expertise calcifying into certainty. It looks like the senior person who has not been corrected in years because nobody bothers any more. It looks like "well, actually", the defensive reply to a code review, the opinion held louder as the evidence for it thins. It looks like learning only from people with more status, and treating every piece of feedback as a negotiation.

## How to get there

- Keep a "Changed my mind" log: date, the belief, what moved it, who deserves the credit. Aim for one entry a month. A year with none means you have stopped looking.
- Be a beginner on schedule. Every year, start one thing you are bad at, under someone's instruction, for at least ninety days. Rotate.
- Reply to criticism with a fixed script: thanks, one clarifying question, what you will change. Nothing else for forty-eight hours.
- Say "I don't know" at least once a day where it is true. Say "I was wrong" within the hour of learning it, to the people who heard the original claim.
- Find the smartest person in the room who disagrees with you and give them the floor. Leave the room with your position written down, and theirs written better.

## Measures

- "Changed my mind" log exists with twelve or more dated entries in the last year.
- Name the skill you are currently a beginner at, who is teaching you, and the date you started. All three answered without hesitation.
- Last public retraction of your own position: dated, within the last ninety days.
- The last three pieces of critical feedback you received: each answered with a change, not a defence, and the change is visible.
