## What it looks like

Every standard in the Body domain is measured in years, not sessions. Training age is counted in unbroken decades, and the program at sixty looks like the program at thirty with the loads adjusted and the warm-up longer. The goal is not a peak; it is a plateau so high and long that decline starts late and moves slowly.

The ceiling reads: training never drops below two sessions a week, in any week, for any reason short of hospitalisation. Travel, illness, a newborn, and a deadline each have a written fallback plan that has already been run. A missed day is followed by a trained day, never a missed week.

Health markers are known cold. Resting heart rate, blood pressure, fasting glucose, HbA1c, lipid panel, VO₂max, and grip strength are each measured on a schedule and trended in one place. Annual physical, dental cleaning twice a year, and every age-appropriate screening booked the year it is due. Family history is written down and shared with the physician.

Vices are not managed; they are absent. No smoking, no vaping, no nicotine. Alcohol is a rare, counted exception. Recreational drugs are not part of the life. Screens, sugar, and gambling are the same mechanism in different clothes and are kept out of the driver's seat.

## The gap

The gap looks like a gym membership used in January and February. It looks like a blood pressure last taken by a nurse years ago, and a physical skipped four years running because nothing hurts. It looks like a pack a week that "doesn't count," wine every night that is "just one," and a training log that ended the week life got busy and never restarted.

## How to get there

- Write the three fallback programs: twenty-minute hotel-room session, sick-day walk and mobility, newborn-week bodyweight circuit. Run each once.
- Define the floor: two sessions a week, no exceptions. Track adherence in the same log as the lifts (see BDY·01).
- Build one health-markers sheet. Enter today's values; calendar the next measurements.
- Book the annual physical and dental cleaning as recurring calendar events, five years out.
- Quit nicotine completely, with help if needed — the highest-leverage single act in the domain. Cap alcohol at a written weekly number (see BDY·05).
- Find training partners or a club. The people beside you at seventy are why you are still there.

## Measures

- Training log shows at least two sessions every week for the last fifty-two weeks.
- Health-markers sheet exists with at least four markers dated within the last twelve months.
- Annual physical completed within the last fourteen months. Dental cleaning within six.
- Nicotine use: zero. Alcohol: every drink in the last ninety days logged and under cap.
- Fallback programs written and each run at least once. Yes or no.

- **Outlive**, Peter Attia — the medicine-of-longevity case for tracking health markers decades before decline, not after.
