## What it looks like

Appearance is the output of BDY·01 through BDY·05, not a goal in itself — but it is measured, because it does not lie.

The ceiling reads: body fat roughly 10–15% for men, 18–24% for women, held year-round rather than peaked for a holiday. Visible muscle at the shoulders, back, and legs, with a waist-to-height ratio under 0.5. A weight known to the kilogram that moves only when told to. Posture upright without effort — head over shoulders, shoulders over hips.

Grooming is part of the standard. Sunscreen whenever the sun is out. Hair, nails, and beard kept in a style chosen rather than defaulted to. Teeth brushed twice and flossed once daily, cleaned professionally twice a year. Clothes fit the body that exists, not the one from five years ago.

## The gap

The gap looks like "big but soft" — strong lifts hidden under twenty kilos nobody is addressing. It looks like the opposite: lean from not eating, with no muscle underneath. It looks like a mirror check that decides whether today is a good day. It looks like a hunched walk, a sunburn every summer, nails bitten to the quick, and a wardrobe of shirts that no longer close at the chest or the belt.

## How to get there

- Measure weight, waist, and a progress photo every month, same day, same light, same time. Date them.
- Run the nutrition numbers from BDY·05 in the direction the measurements demand: a slow cut at a half-kilo a week, or a slow gain at a quarter-kilo a week. Never both, never neither.
- Keep strength training through every cut. Muscle is the thing that makes the result look like anything.
- Fix posture in the gym — rows, face pulls, carries, hip hinges — and at the desk (see BDY·03).
- Dentist and dermatologist on the calendar for the year.
- Take every item of clothing that does not fit to the tailor or the donation bin this month.

## Measures

- Monthly waist, weight, and photo logged for the last twelve months.
- Waist-to-height ratio under 0.5. Measured, not estimated.
- Body fat estimate within the ceiling range on the last measurement.
- Dental cleaning within the last six months; dermatology check within the last year if over thirty-five.
- Zero garments in the wardrobe that do not fit today. Yes or no.

## Canon

- **The Renaissance Diet 2.0**, Mike Israetel, Melissa Davis & James Hoffmann — the cutting-and-bulking framework behind sustainable body-composition change without wrecking training.
- **Bigger Leaner Stronger** / **Thinner Leaner Stronger**, Michael Matthews — accessible, evidence-based body-composition standards for men and women respectively.
- **ACSM's Guidelines for Exercise Testing and Prescription**, American College of Sports Medicine — the clinical reference behind the body-fat and health-marker ranges cited throughout this domain.
- **Waist-to-Height Ratio: A Simple Geometric Index**, Margaret Ashwell — the paper that established the under-0.5 waist-to-height threshold as a health marker.
