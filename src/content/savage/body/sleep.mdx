## What it looks like

Training is the stimulus; sleep is the adaptation. The savage treats it as the highest-priority appointment of the day, because it is.

The ceiling reads: seven to nine hours of actual sleep, not time in bed, every night. Bedtime and wake time fixed within thirty minutes, seven days a week — weekends included. Waking before the alarm most mornings. Falling asleep within twenty minutes. Waking at most once a night.

The environment is engineered: the room is cold, dark enough that a hand in front of the face is invisible, and quiet or masked. No screen is in the bedroom. The last caffeine of the day is ten hours before bed, the last meal three, the last alcohol none (see BDY·07). The hour before bed is the same every night — dim light, no work, no feed.

Ten minutes of outdoor light within an hour of waking anchors the clock. Training ends early enough that the nervous system has come down by bedtime.

## The gap

The gap looks like "I function fine on six." It looks like a phone charging on the nightstand and a last scroll at 1 a.m. It looks like sleeping in on Saturday to repay a debt that cannot be repaid, then a Sunday night that will not come. It looks like a pre-workout at 4 p.m., a nightcap to "take the edge off," and snoring nobody has mentioned to a doctor.

## How to get there

- Pick a wake time. Hold it for thirty days without exception. Bedtime follows from it: wake time minus eight and a half hours.
- Strip the bedroom: blackout blinds or a mask, temperature around 18 °C, phone outside the door. Buy the blinds this week.
- Write down a caffeine cutoff and an alcohol cutoff, measured against bedtime.
- Build a fixed thirty-minute wind-down. Same order every night.
- Track sleep for ninety days — wearable or a paper log — and correct the single worst variable the data shows.
- Loud snoring, gasping, or waking unrefreshed after eight hours gets a sleep study. Book it; do not self-diagnose.

## Measures

- Ninety-day log shows seven-plus hours on at least eighty-five nights.
- Wake time varies by no more than thirty minutes across all seven days of the week.
- Alarm-free waking on the majority of mornings in the last month.
- No screen in the bedroom. Yes or no.
- Last caffeine timestamp is ten or more hours before bedtime, every day in the log.

## Canon

- **Why We Sleep**, Matthew Walker — the case for sleep as a biological non-negotiable, with the cost of shortchanging it laid out in full.
- **The Sleep Solution**, W. Chris Winter — a sleep-medicine physician's practical breakdown of sleep architecture, disorders, and what actually fixes bad sleep.
- **Circadian Code**, Satchin Panda — the science of light exposure and timing as the mechanism that anchors the sleep-wake clock.
- **Principles and Practice of Sleep Medicine**, Meir Kryger, Thomas Roth & William Dement — the clinical reference behind sleep-study thresholds and diagnosis.
