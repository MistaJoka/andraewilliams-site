## What it looks like

Strength without range is a cage. The savage squats to full depth with heels down, reaches overhead with a straight spine, touches the floor flat-palmed, sits cross-legged or in a deep squat for ten minutes without fidgeting, and rotates the hips and thoracic spine freely in both directions.

Durability is the other half: the body does not break under load it should handle. Tendons are conditioned. Ankles, knees, hips, shoulders and wrists are trained through their ranges with load, not just stretched. Old injuries are rehabbed to full function, not managed forever.

It shows up as never being the person who "threw their back out" picking up a bag. It shows up at fifty when peers are having hips replaced.

## The gap

The gap is stretching as an afterthought, or not at all. It is a deep squat that needs a plate under the heels and a press that arches the lower back to make up for stuck shoulders. It is a list of "bad" joints that dictate what is and is not trained. It is stiffness accepted as a normal part of getting older.

## How to get there

- Ten to fifteen minutes of daily mobility, every day, non-negotiable. Loaded end-range work beats passive stretching: Cossack squats, Jefferson curls, controlled articular rotations, hanging.
- Spend time on the floor. Sit in a deep squat. Sleep in positions that do not wreck the neck.
- Train through full range under load in the main lifts; range built with weight sticks.
- Treat pain as data. Get it assessed, rehab it properly, return to full function. Do not route around it for a decade.

## Measures

- Full-depth bodyweight squat, heels down, held for two minutes: yes or no.
- Palms flat on the floor with straight legs: yes or no.
- Overhead reach with ribs down and a neutral spine: yes or no.
- Number of movements currently avoided due to pain. The target is zero.

## Canon

- **Becoming a Supple Leopard**, Kelly Starrett — the field guide for treating mobility and joint mechanics as trainable, not fixed.
- **Athletic Body in Balance**, Gray Cook — the movement-screen logic for spotting and fixing asymmetries before they become injuries.
- **Rebuilding Milo**, Aaron Horschig — a rehab-and-durability framework built specifically around returning lifters to full range under load.
- **Anatomy Trains**, Thomas Myers — the myofascial-connectivity model behind why range of motion in one joint affects the whole chain.
