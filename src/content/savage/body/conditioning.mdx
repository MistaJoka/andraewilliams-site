## What it looks like

Conditioning is the capacity to keep going. The savage has a deep aerobic base — hours of easy effort available on demand — and a sharp top end that can be called on when it matters. Resting heart rate sits in the fifties or lower. Recovery between hard efforts is fast and visible.

The practical ceiling: run five kilometres comfortably under twenty-five minutes, hold a conversational pace for ninety minutes, cover a mile in under seven, and carry a third of your bodyweight for an hour without collapsing afterwards. Swim a few hundred metres without panic. Ride a bike for a day.

It shows up as never being the person who has to stop. It shows up in recovery from illness, from surgery, from a bad week — the base is what you fall back on.

## The gap

The gap is lifting heavy and gasping after one flight of stairs. It is "cardio kills gains" said by someone who has never tested either claim. It is a single mode — only running, only the bike — so the body is fragile outside that one groove. It is zone-two work skipped because it is boring, and every session done at the same middling, unproductive intensity.

## How to get there

- Build the base first: three to five hours per week of easy, conversational effort — running, rowing, cycling, rucking, swimming. Most of your conditioning volume lives here.
- Add one or two hard sessions a week once the base is in: intervals, hill repeats, a tempo effort. Short, measured, recovered from.
- Rotate modes so the body is capable, not specialised. Rucking and swimming expose weaknesses running hides.
- Track resting heart rate and heart-rate recovery. When they stall, the problem is sleep or volume, not motivation.

## Measures

- 5 km time, mile time, and resting heart rate, all dated within the last ninety days.
- Weekly easy-effort hours, logged.
- Can you complete a ninety-minute conversational effort this week, today, without preparation? If no, the base has slipped.
