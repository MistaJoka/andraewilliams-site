## What it looks like

The savage has a default diet that runs without willpower, because the decisions were made once and the environment enforces them.

The ceiling reads: protein at 1.6–2.2 g per kilogram of bodyweight, every day, spread across three or four feedings. The rest of the plate is mostly food that had a face, a root, or a leaf — vegetables at most meals, fruit daily, whole grains and legumes over refined ones, fat from fish, olives, nuts, and eggs. Fibre at thirty grams or more. Water until urine is pale. Ultra-processed food is an exception, not a category.

Calories are known, not guessed. The savage knows their maintenance intake within two hundred calories and can gain, hold, or cut on purpose (see BDY·06). Alcohol is treated as what it is — a recovery tax with no nutritional upside — and kept rare (see BDY·07).

The cooking is done at home, to a list, from a rotation of ten meals that need no thought. The kitchen is stocked so the easy choice is the correct one.

## The gap

The gap looks like protein hit by accident on a good day and missed on most. It looks like a "clean" diet with no idea of the numbers, and a weight that drifts three kilos a year in whichever direction. It looks like eating out five nights a week, snacking from a bag because it was on the counter, and a supplement shelf doing the job a grocery list should.

## How to get there

- Track every meal for thirty days — long enough to learn what a hundred grams of protein and two thousand calories look like. Then eat from knowledge.
- Protein first at every meal. Build the plate around it; add the rest.
- Write the ten-meal rotation. Shop for it weekly. Cook in batches twice a week.
- Remove ultra-processed food from the house. The discipline is at the store, not the cupboard.
- Shopping and batch-cooking are fixed weekly calendar slots, not things that happen when there is time.
- Hold alcohol to a written weekly cap. Zero is the ceiling.

## Measures

- Thirty-day tracked log exists and is dated within the last year.
- Maintenance calories known and written down; bodyweight held within two kilograms of target for ninety days.
- Protein target hit on six or more days per week, checked by log.
- Home-cooked meals outnumber restaurant and takeaway meals in any given week.
- Weekly alcohol count logged and under cap for the last three months.

## Canon

- **The Muscle and Strength Nutrition Pyramid**, Eric Helms, Andy Morgan & Andrea Valdez — the evidence-ranked hierarchy of what matters in a performance diet, from calories down to supplements.
- **How Not to Diet**, Michael Greger — a data-heavy look at food quality and energy balance from the whole-food end of the spectrum.
- **Advanced Nutrition and Human Metabolism**, Sareen Gropper & Jack Smith — the textbook-grade reference for protein, fibre, and micronutrient requirements cited here.
- **In Defense of Food**, Michael Pollan — the plain-language case for whole food over ultra-processed food as the default.
