## What it looks like

The savage stands tall without stiffness: ears over shoulders, shoulders over hips, weight on both feet, chin level. Sitting is the same line with the spine off the backrest. Walking is unhurried and direct — no shuffle, no bounce, no scanning the floor. The head is up and the eyes are on the world.

Stillness is the tell. Hands rest when they are not being used. No pen-clicking, leg-bouncing, hair-touching, phone-checking, collar-adjusting. In conversation the savage faces the other person squarely and does not fidget toward the exit. Movements are slower than the room's, and the room slows to match.

Handshake is firm, dry, two seconds, with eye contact. Eye contact generally is held three to five seconds at a time, broken to the side and not down. Personal space is respected and occupied without apology. The savage takes up exactly the room a person takes up — no shrinking, no sprawling.

## The gap

The gap is the phone-neck hunch that has become the default shape. It is the apologetic entrance, the half-standing hover at the edge of a group, the seat taken at the back. It is arms crossed or hands in pockets for the entire conversation. It is the jittery knee, the glance at the watch, the eyes that keep dropping to the floor. It is the dead-fish handshake or the crushing one, both trying to prove something. It is the body saying "I would rather not be here" while the mouth says otherwise.

## How to get there

- Fix the structure first. Posture is mobility and strength, not willpower: thoracic extension, hip flexor length, a strong posterior chain (see BDY·01, BDY·03). Ten minutes daily of targeted work.
- Set a silent hourly check for two weeks: where are your ears, shoulders, chin? Correct each time. The habit installs.
- Film yourself walking into a room and sitting down, from across the space. Watch it. Fix the two worst things. Repeat monthly.
- Pick one fidget and eliminate it completely for thirty days before moving to the next.
- Practise stillness on purpose: ten minutes sitting upright, hands resting, without moving. Daily.
- Enter every room as if it was expecting you. Walk to the centre or the front. Sit in the chair that faces the door.

## Measures

- Side-profile photo, standing relaxed, taken this quarter: ear, shoulder, hip, and ankle in a vertical line.
- Zero identified fidgets remaining on a dated self-review video from the last ninety days.
- Ten minutes of motionless upright sitting, logged daily for the last thirty days.
- Handshake, eye contact, and stance rated by a blunt friend or coach within the last six months, with notes.
