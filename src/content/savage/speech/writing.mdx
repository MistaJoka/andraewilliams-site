## What it looks like

The savage writes the way the savage speaks: short sentences, concrete nouns, active verbs, the point first. A message opens with the ask or the answer. A document opens with the conclusion, then the reasoning, then the evidence.

Spelling, grammar, and punctuation are correct every time, in a text message as much as in a contract. Formatting carries meaning: headings that can be skimmed, lists when items are parallel, one idea per paragraph. Numbers are exact. Dates include the year. Names are spelled the way their owners spell them.

Length matches purpose. A chat reply is one line. An email is five sentences or it becomes a document. A document is as long as the decision requires and not a word more. The savage can produce a clean one-page brief on an unfamiliar topic in under an hour and a polished long-form piece — 2,000 words, structured, cited — in a working day.

## The gap

The gap is the email that starts with "Hope you're well!" and gets to the point in paragraph four. It is "per my last email" and "just circling back". It is bullet points with no verbs, a question buried in the middle of a paragraph, and three asks in one message so that none gets answered. It is passive voice hiding who did what. It is jargon as a substitute for understanding.

## How to get there

- Conclusion first. Every message, every document. If the reader stops after line one, they should still know what to do.
- Write the draft, then cut it by a third. Then read it aloud and cut what you stumbled on.
- One ask per message. If there are three, send three, or write a document with three numbered questions.
- Ban the list: "just", "very", "really", "in order to", "I think", "hopefully", "going forward", "reach out". Search for them before sending.
- Read at least one book on prose per year and one short piece of excellent writing per week.
- Write something every day — 300 words minimum — whether anyone reads it or not. Volume is the only route to speed.

## Measures

- Zero spelling or grammar errors in the last thirty messages you sent — check them.
- Every email sent this week has the ask or answer in its first sentence.
- Writing log: 300+ words per day, dated, no gap longer than seven days in the last year.
- A clean one-page brief on a new topic, produced in under sixty minutes, dated within the last quarter.
