## What it looks like

The savage speaks in complete sentences and stops when the sentence is done. There is no "um", no "like", no "sort of", no "I just think maybe". A question gets an answer — "yes", "no", "Thursday", "I don't know, I'll find out by noon" — and then the air goes quiet, and the savage is comfortable in it.

Fewer words carry more weight. A request is one sentence. A disagreement is stated once, plainly, with the reason attached. A promise is specific: what, by when, to whom. When there is nothing to add, nothing is added. People leave a conversation with the savage knowing exactly what was said, because exactly what was said is what was meant.

Precision extends to register. The same thought is delivered to a plumber, a board, and a child in three different vocabularies, and none of them is a dumbed-down or dressed-up version of the truth.

## The gap

The gap is talking to be heard rather than to be understood. It is the three-minute answer to a yes/no question. It is "I feel like maybe we should possibly look at" when the meaning is "do this". It is sarcasm as a default setting, jokes to deflect every serious moment, and the nervous habit of ending statements as questions. It is interrupting, re-explaining, and repeating a point because the silence after the first delivery was uncomfortable. It is saying "I'll try" when the honest word is "no".

## How to get there

- Record yourself in three ordinary conversations or calls. Count fillers per minute. The ceiling is under one. Keep recording monthly until it is.
- Practise the full stop. Answer, then close your mouth. Two seconds of silence is not your problem to solve.
- Strip hedges from your vocabulary: "just", "kind of", "I think maybe", "sort of", "honestly". Replace with the claim or with silence.
- Every commitment gets a deadline and a deliverable in the same breath. "I'll look into it" becomes "I'll send you the numbers by Tuesday."
- Before anything important, write the one sentence you need to land. Say that sentence first.
- Swear only on purpose. A word used every minute has no force left when you need it.

## Measures

- Filler count under one per minute on a dated recording in the last ninety days.
- Zero commitments made this month without a stated deadline.
- Someone can repeat back your main point after any meeting you ran — check by asking.
- You can name the last time you said "no" flatly, without an apology attached, within the last week.
