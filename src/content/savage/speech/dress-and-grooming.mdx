## What it looks like

The savage is never under- or over-dressed. Funeral, boardroom, building site, beach wedding, 6 a.m. flight — the right clothes are owned, clean, and ready. Everything fits: shoulders sit on the shoulders, sleeves end at the wrist bone, trousers break once or not at all, nothing pulls, nothing billows. Fit is the whole game.

Grooming is daily and invisible. Hair cut on a schedule, not when it gets noticed. Nails short and clean. Skin cared for. Teeth white and breath neutral. Facial hair either deliberate and maintained or absent. Scent, if any, noticed only by someone standing close.

The wardrobe is small and complete. Every item works with most of the others. Shoes are polished and resoled, not replaced. Clothes are hung, folded, repaired when torn, and retired when they fade. Packing for a week with three kinds of occasion takes fifteen minutes, because the system is already built.

## The gap

The gap is the hoodie at a dinner that called for a jacket, or the suit that screams at a barbecue. It is the shirt that pulls across the chest and the trousers pooling on the shoes. It is a closet full of things that do not go together and an "I have nothing to wear" every important morning. It is the hair that got long because nobody booked the appointment, the scuffed shoes under a good suit, the frayed collar, the nails that tell a story.

## How to get there

- Audit the wardrobe in one afternoon. Every piece: fits perfectly, goes with at least three others, and has been worn this year — or it leaves.
- Find a tailor. Take every new garment and every existing keeper.
- Build a palette of three neutrals and one accent. Everything bought must belong to it.
- Book haircuts as a standing appointment — every three to five weeks — and never cancel for convenience.
- Daily: shower, deodorant, teeth twice, skin. Weekly: nails, shoe polish, iron or steam what needs it. Monthly: laundry of anything that sits, repairs, rotate seasonals.
- Learn the dress codes cold: black tie, cocktail, business, smart casual, casual. Own one correct outfit for each and know which the invitation meant.

## Measures

- Wardrobe inventory exists, dated this year, and every listed item has been worn in the last twelve months.
- Zero garments owned that do not fit as bought or as altered.
- Haircut log: no gap longer than five weeks in the last year.
- You can dress correctly for any of the five standard dress codes tonight, from what is in the closet right now — yes or no.

## Canon

- **Dressing the Man**, Alan Flusser — permanent principles of fit and proportion, over trend.
- **The Curated Closet**, Anuschka Rees — building a small, versatile, gender-neutral wardrobe as a system.
- **Gentleman: A Timeless Guide to Fashion**, Bernhard Roetzel — dress codes and correct dress for every occasion, explained plainly.
