## What it looks like

The savage listens with the whole body. Phone face down or in another room. Eyes on the speaker. No rehearsing a reply while the other person is still talking. When they finish, the savage can repeat the substance back in their words, not a paraphrase that slides toward what the savage wanted to hear.

Listening is active. Questions go one layer deeper than the obvious — "what happened next", "what did you want to happen", "what are you not saying". The savage notices the sentence that was started and abandoned, the name that was avoided, the shift in tone at minute four. Silence is used as a tool: most people fill a pause with the thing they were holding back.

Recall is part of the standard. Names, children's names, the project someone mentioned three months ago, the thing they were worried about — retained and referenced. People feel remembered because they are.

## The gap

The gap is waiting to talk. It is the nod that means "hurry up". It is checking a notification mid-sentence and saying "go on, I'm listening". It is answering the question you expected instead of the one asked. It is solving a problem someone only wanted witnessed, and missing the problem they actually brought. It is forgetting a name the second time you meet, and forgetting the thing they told you mattered to them.

## How to get there

- Phone out of sight in every conversation that matters. Not face down. Gone.
- Before replying to anything with stakes, restate what you heard in one sentence and ask "is that right?" Do this until it is a reflex.
- Ask one more question than feels necessary. Then one more.
- Count to three in your head before responding to anything emotional. Most of what you would have said in that window was about you.
- Keep a private note after significant conversations: what they said, what they cared about, what you owe them. Review before the next meeting.
- Learn every name on first hearing. Use it within the first minute. If you missed it, ask again immediately — not ten minutes later.

## Measures

- You can write down the three main points of the last meeting you attended without notes.
- Zero instances this week of someone saying "that's not what I said" or "as I mentioned".
- Names of every person you met in the last month, recalled on demand.
- A dated conversation log exists for your ten most important relationships and was updated within thirty days.
