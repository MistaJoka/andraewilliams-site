## What it looks like

The savage thinks in decades and acts in days. There is a written ten-year direction — not a fantasy, a direction — with a three-year plan under it and a twelve-month plan under that. Every major choice is checked against it: the job, the city, the relationship, the investment, the skill to learn next.

It looks like compounding taken seriously in every domain. Money left to grow for thirty years. A craft practised for ten thousand hours without switching lanes. Friendships maintained across decades. A reputation built one kept promise at a time. The savage understands that the first years of anything compounding look like nothing, and holds the line through that stretch when most people quit.

It looks like patience that is indistinguishable from stubbornness to outsiders, and like luck to people who only see the results.

## The gap

The gap is living quarter to quarter. Switching careers at year three, right before competence. Selling at the bottom, chasing the trend, restarting the plan every January. Having a vague sense of "someday" instead of a dated direction. Optimising each week for comfort and wondering at forty why nothing accumulated.

## How to get there

- Write the ten-year direction on one page: who you are at the end of it, what exists that does not exist now, what you will have stopped doing. Date it.
- Derive a three-year plan and a twelve-month plan from it. Review all three on a fixed date each year; revise the direction only for real reasons, never for a bad month.
- Pick the three things you will compound — one skill, one asset, one set of relationships — and refuse to switch them for at least five years.
- Automate the compounding where possible: contributions, practice blocks, standing calls. Willpower is not a long-term strategy.
- Make decisions with a "year ten" test: will this matter, help, or embarrass you in ten years? Weight that over the next quarter.
- Keep a dated record of the big decisions and the reasoning, so you can learn from the ones that were wrong.

## Measures

- A written, dated ten-year direction exists and is less than twelve months since last review.
- Three-year and twelve-month plans exist and trace to it, line by line.
- The three compounding commitments are named and each has at least five unbroken years, or a dated start.
- Every decision above a personal threshold (job, home, major capital) in the last three years is logged with its reasoning.
- No restart of the primary plan in the last five years.

## Canon

- **The Compound Effect**, Darren Hardy — the mechanics of small, consistent decisions accumulating into outsized results over years.
- **The Psychology of Money**, Morgan Housel — why patience and long time horizons beat skill in compounding wealth and decisions alike.
- **The Almanack of Naval Ravikant**, Eric Jorgenson — compounding applied across wealth, skill, and relationships as one unified philosophy.
- **The Gap and The Gain**, Dan Sullivan & Benjamin Hardy — measuring progress against where you started instead of an ever-receding ideal.
