## What it looks like

The savage teaches on purpose. Not occasionally, not when asked twice, but as a standing part of how they operate. Knowledge that stays in one head is a liability; the ceiling is that anything you know how to do, at least one other person has learned from you.

It looks like a mentee or apprentice at all times — a junior at work, a younger relative, someone from outside your circle who asked. It looks like explanations built for the learner rather than the ego of the explainer: the principle first, a worked example, then the learner doing it with you watching, then alone. It looks like the patience to let someone fail on something cheap so they do not fail on something expensive.

It looks like pride when a person you taught outstrips you, and a public record of it — you name them, you recommend them, you put them in front of the people who can move their career. The measure of your teaching is their independence from you, not their loyalty to you.

## The gap

The gap is hoarding. The expert who keeps the knowledge vague so they stay necessary. The senior who answers every question with the answer instead of the method, and builds a team that cannot move without them. The mentor in name who meets twice and vanishes. The teacher who explains once, fast, and decides the learner is slow.

## How to get there

- Keep at least one formal mentee at all times. Fixed cadence, written goals, a review every quarter on what they can now do that they could not do before.
- Teach in the sequence: explain, demonstrate, supervise, release. Do not skip the supervised stage and do not linger in it.
- Answer "how do I" with the method plus the answer. Show the search, the doc, the reasoning, so the second question never comes.
- Write down anything you have explained three times. Hand the document over instead of the fourth explanation.
- Recommend people out loud: introductions, references, nominations. One per month, minimum.
- Let them take the work you are best at. That is the work they will learn the most from, and the work you most need to stop owning.

## Measures

- A named mentee exists today with a written goal list and a dated quarterly review.
- At least one skill you hold has been fully transferred in the last year — the other person does it unsupervised.
- Introductions or recommendations made: count them, monthly, minimum one.
- Someone you taught has surpassed you in at least one area, and you can name them and the area.
