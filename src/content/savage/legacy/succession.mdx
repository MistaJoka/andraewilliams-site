## What it looks like

The savage works to make themselves unnecessary. Every role held — at work, at home, in the community — has someone who could step in, and that person has actually done the job while you watched. Being irreplaceable is not a compliment; it is a failure of planning.

It looks like deliberate handover: the successor runs the thing for a week while you are reachable, then a month while you are not. It looks like a written handover file for every responsibility, current enough that a competent stranger could pick it up. It looks like shared access to every account, key, and document that matters, with no single password living only in your head.

It looks like household succession too. Your partner or a named adult knows where the money is, what the bills are, how the house works, and what to do the day you are gone. A will exists, is current, and the people named in it know they are named.

It looks like leaving every role cleanly when the time comes — no lingering, no undermining the person who took over.

## The gap

The gap is the hero. The only one who knows how the system works and finds that flattering. The manager with no deputy. The parent whose partner could not find the insurance policy. The founder who grooms no successor and takes the institution with them. The departed leader who keeps calling to second-guess the replacement.

## How to get there

- List every responsibility you hold. Name a successor for each. Where none exists, that is the first job.
- Write a handover document per role: what it is, how it runs, who to call, where the access is. Review it twice a year.
- Put the successor in the seat: one week with you available, then one full week with you unreachable. Fix what breaks.
- Consolidate access into a shared, secured system — a password manager with an emergency contact, documented keys, named signatories. Nothing lives only in your head.
- Keep a current will and an emergency file: accounts, policies, contacts, instructions. Walk one named person through it once a year.
- When you leave a role, leave it. Thirty days of availability for questions, then silence unless asked.

## Measures

- Every responsibility on the list has a named successor, and the list was reviewed within the last six months.
- Each successor has run the role unsupervised for at least one full week, dated.
- A handover document exists per role, updated within the last twelve months.
- A will exists, is less than three years old, and the named person has seen the emergency file in the last year.
- Zero accounts, keys, or passwords known only to you — audit annually.
