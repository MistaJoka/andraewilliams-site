## What it looks like

Most work evaporates when its maker stops. The savage builds at least some things designed to keep going without them: a company, a team, a process, a body of work, a community institution, a piece of infrastructure, a family practice. The test is simple — remove yourself for a year and see whether it is still standing.

It looks like things built for the next operator, not just the current one. Decisions written down with their reasons. Systems that need no hero. Ownership that is named and transferable. Funding, maintenance, and governance planned for a horizon beyond your involvement. It looks like choosing a slower build with a deeper foundation over a faster one that depends on your personal attention.

It looks like at least one thing with a decade behind it. Longevity is the evidence; anyone can launch.

## The gap

The gap is the serial starter — five projects launched, none past year two. It is the founder who is the single point of failure and proud of it. It is the undocumented system, the org that collapses when its leader moves on, the charity that runs on one person's weekends. It is building for applause at launch instead of for use in year ten.

## How to get there

- Pick one thing to build for a twenty-year horizon and name it. Write the page that says what it is for and what it must never become.
- Design for your absence from day one: named owners, written procedures, access that does not route through you.
- Fund and staff maintenance explicitly. Anything living needs a budget line for keeping it alive.
- Put governance in place before it is needed: who decides when you are gone, and how.
- Finish things. Kill the projects that will not get the next ten years of attention, and pour that attention into the ones that will.
- Review the build every year against the founding page. Drift is fixed early or it becomes the identity.

## Measures

- At least one thing you built is more than ten years old and in active use.
- At least one thing you built has operated for a full year without your hands on it.
- A written founding document exists for your primary long-term build, reviewed and dated within the last twelve months.
- Every critical system you own has a named successor owner who has actually run it.
- Projects abandoned before year two in the last five years: fewer than the projects still running.

## Canon

- **Built to Last**, Jim Collins & Jerry Porras — the study of companies engineered to survive their founders, and what actually made that possible.
- **Good to Great**, Jim Collins — the research behind Level 5 leadership and organisations built to keep performing after the builder leaves.
- **The Effective Executive**, Peter Drucker — the discipline of building institutions and decisions that hold up without the founder's daily hand.
- **Team of Teams**, Stanley McChrystal — building decentralised systems that keep functioning without a single point of command.
