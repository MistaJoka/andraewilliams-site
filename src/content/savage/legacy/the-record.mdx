## What it looks like

Memory dies with the person. Writing does not. The savage keeps a record — of the work, the decisions, the lessons, and the people — and keeps it in a form that will still be readable in fifty years. It is the difference between a life that teaches the next generation and one they reconstruct from rumours.

It looks like a journal with years of entries. A decision log: the call, the reasoning, the outcome, what it taught. A short written set of personal principles, revised as they are tested. Letters written to specific people — children, mentees, a partner — to be read later. The professional body of work published where it can be found, not locked in a drive that will be wiped when the account closes.

It looks like plain formats and redundant copies. Text files, printed pages, an archive in two places. It looks like an index, so that anyone inheriting the record can find the part they need.

## The gap

The gap is the wise person who never wrote anything down, whose family remembers only a few phrases. It is the decision log that lives in someone's head until the head is gone. It is the hard drive with no backup, the proprietary app that shuts down, the thousand notes with no structure. It is the letter to the kids that was always going to be written next year.

## How to get there

- Journal at a fixed cadence — daily or weekly — and keep the entries. Quantity beats eloquence; the value is in the years.
- Log every significant decision with date, reasoning, and expected outcome. Revisit each one a year later and write what actually happened.
- Write your principles on one page. Revise them when reality breaks one, and keep the old versions.
- Write one letter per year to each person who matters most, dated and stored where they will receive it.
- Publish the work: the craft, the methods, the lessons. A public record is the hardest to lose.
- Archive in plain text or paper, in two locations, with an index file at the top that says what is where. Test the restore once a year.

## Measures

- Journal entries exist for at least forty of the last fifty-two weeks.
- Decision log has entries for every major decision in the last three years, with one-year follow-ups written.
- A personal principles document exists, dated, with at least one prior version kept.
- Letters written in the last twelve months: one per named person, stored and findable.
- Archive exists in two locations, in open formats, with an index; restore tested within the last year.

## Canon

- **Meditations**, Marcus Aurelius — a private decision-and-principles log that survived two thousand years, proof the form works.
- **The Autobiography of Benjamin Franklin**, Benjamin Franklin — a written record of principles and lessons deliberately left for those who came after.
- **Show Your Work!**, Austin Kleon — the case for publishing the work and the process instead of locking it away.
- **How to Take Smart Notes**, Sönke Ahrens — a concrete system for keeping a written archive that stays usable and findable over decades.
