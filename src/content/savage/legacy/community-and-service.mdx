## What it looks like

The savage is a load-bearing member of a community. Someone with a role that would be missed. A coach, a board member, a volunteer with a standing shift, the person the neighbours call when something needs doing, the one who organises the thing everyone else only attends.

It looks like time given on a schedule, because service that waits for spare time never happens. It looks like giving your strongest skill, not your leftover hours — the accountant doing the books for the club, the builder fixing the hall, the teacher running the after-school programme. It looks like money given deliberately, as a fixed percentage, decided once and automated rather than reconsidered every time.

It looks like knowing the people around you by name — neighbours, the shop owners, the people who run the local institutions — and being known by them.

## The gap

The gap is the person who "would love to help" and never has a Tuesday free. It is the occasional donation that replaces involvement. It is the person who moved in four years ago and cannot name three neighbours. It is service for the photo and the post. It is the assumption that someone else will run the thing.

## How to get there

- Take one standing role in one community institution, with a recurring commitment on the calendar. Keep it for years, not months.
- Give a fixed percentage of income to causes you have chosen; set it up as a standing transfer and review the recipients once a year.
- Offer your primary professional skill pro bono to one organisation at a time, with a defined scope and an end date.
- Learn the names of everyone within ten doors, and the people who run the places you go weekly.
- Show up for the hard days: the work party, the hospital visit, the move, the funeral. Absence is noticed more than presence.
- Build one thing for the community that was not there before — an event, a group, a resource — and hand it off when it is stable.

## Measures

- A named standing role exists, with a calendar commitment kept in at least forty-five of the last fifty-two weeks.
- Giving is a fixed percentage, automated, and the number is known cold.
- A current pro bono engagement with written scope exists, or one was completed in the last twelve months.
- You can name ten neighbours and the people who run five local institutions.
- Hours of service logged in the last year: a number, not a guess.
